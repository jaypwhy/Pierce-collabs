## 1. COHORT CHARTER

### 1.1 What a seed cohort IS

A **seed cohort** is a small starter group of **5–25 participants** in **one pocket of the City of San Diego**. It is not a program tier, not a waiting room, and not a pilot with an end date. It is one complete delivery of the whole system at human scale, small enough that every participant is a known person to a named team.

Each cohort has exactly five defining parts:

1. **One pocket.** A walkable area of the City of San Diego — never the County, never split across two neighborhoods. The pocket is chosen so that transit, food, health, hygiene, and pet care already exist within reach (see §2.2).
2. **One housing arrangement.** Either a **single housing cluster** (a building or a set of units on one site) or **distributed units** scattered inside the same pocket. The choice is per pocket, driven by what real estate is actually available and pet-friendly.
3. **One case-owner team.** The four named roles in §3, with **exactly one named case-owner attached to each participant's payment file** — no orphan files, no "everyone thought someone else sent it."
4. **The whole six-pillar system at once.** Housing, health, food, hygiene, regenerative income, and zero-entropy travel arrive together, as one navigable system — never as a list of referrals for the participant to work through alone. Wellness animals arrive with their people from day one.
5. **One governance body.** Every participant in the cohort is a voting member of the **cohort council**, seated as soon as cohort 1 opens (§7).

### 1.2 Charter clauses (the rules that define membership)

- **Eligibility is need, not merit.** The cohort enrolls people who are unsheltered or housing-insecure in the City of San Diego. There is no readiness test, no sobriety requirement, no "housing-ready" score, no work requirement in either participation model (§4), and no behavioral condition on housing.
- **Enrollment is opt-out, not opt-in.** The team identifies and enrolls eligible people — **the paperwork is done by the team, not by the participant** (§5).
- **Every participant gets the same base package.** §4 lays out both participation models side by side and they are **identical in every material line**: same housing quality, same cleaning and connectivity rights, same pet setup and pet care, same flexible stipend, same access to wellness-animal accommodations, same autism-accommodation build specs. The difference between the models is *only* whether regenerative-income pathways are active. It is never a difference in what people receive.
- **Cleaning services and connectivity are RIGHTS.** Permanent budget lines in every cohort and every model — never removed, never reduced, never used as a lever.
- **Care & education supplies are a permanent program-funded line.** Caretakers — kinship, elder, and child caregivers — and teachers, professors, trainers, and educational staff working inside our system **never pay for the tools of their work out of pocket.** Supplies, training and classroom equipment, care consumables for the person cared for, activity and learning materials, and transport to care or teaching commitments are **program-funded**. The line is **never deducted from anyone's personal stipend**, is never conditional on performance, and is **permanent** in the same spirit as cleaning and connectivity (owner directive 2026-10-01). It is **not** one of the two locked RIGHTS today — **the owner may elevate it later**; it stands as a permanent program-funded line (see Amendment A §5). The reason, in the owner's words: educational professionals told the owner directly that paying for classroom supplies out of their own pockets is a huge frustration of the profession — and care work is work, so the tools of care are never the caregiver's private expense.
- **Wellness animals are requirements, not extras.** Pet-friendly housing is an acquisition, lease, and renovation requirement. Pet setup and monthly pet care are in every cohort budget. Settling-in padding covers people **and** animals.
- **Nobody carries their own transition alone.** Movement and settling-in support is offered **proactively at every transition** — first move-in, pocket transfers, DV safety transfers, household splits, upgrades, any unit change — and **declining it costs nothing, is recorded nowhere, and changes nothing.** Transitions are program work, not participant burden. It is **not** a third locked RIGHT: it is a permanent program-funded service in the spirit of cleaning and connectivity, and **the owner may elevate it later** (Amendment B §3).
- **Housing rights do not sunset.** Regeneration is not an exit ramp. There is no date at which a participant is expected to "graduate out" of a home. The program's job is permanent stability, not throughput.
- **Backpay posture.** The Future Fund accommodates people displaced 20+ years ago by circumstances beyond their control, as **present-tense relief for current housing need with documented long-ago displacement as a prioritization tier** — race-neutral, inside an open charitable class. It is not literal retroactive compensation, which a 501(c)(3) generally cannot pay; counsel shapes the criteria before any money moves. The quad-directive headroom, the flexible stipend, settling-in padding, and lagniappe as the default posture are how that shows up in practice.

### 1.3 Cohort lifecycle

Framework planning durations — operating guidance, not verified timelines.

| Stage | Window (framework estimate) | What happens | Who owns it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Formation** | 60–120 days before first move-in | Pocket confirmed by site walk; units secured and taken through master-framework Phases 1–2 (secure/safe, wet rooms); partner agreements signed (shelter/housing, food, health, transit, animal welfare); case-owner team hired; all eight error controls standing up, with the five non-negotiables live before dollar one; cohort council framework drafted | Operations coordinator + community liaison |
| **Zero-barrier onboarding** | Days 0–30 | Enrollment by the team; phone-verified enrollment; test payment confirmed; unit handover to the accommodation standards; 30-day buddy check + mentor assigned (per the source onboarding checklist); payment calendar published; pet care and vet baselines set | Cohort navigator / case-owner |
| **Stabilization** | Months 1–12 | Base needs and UI flowing uninterrupted on a published calendar; monthly participant-confirmation of every disbursement; monthly feedback sessions; monthly error/fix report (including zeros); first honest retention data collected for the wave gate | Case-owner team + shared ops |
| **Regeneration** | Month 12 onward — **no end date** | Regenerative-income pathways active (or declined, freely); participant cushion builds; cohort governance matured; contributions celebrated publicly; surplus may flow to the commons if and when the participant chooses | Regenerative-income coach + cohort council |

Non-negotiable rule across all four stages: **if any non-negotiable error control lapses, new disbursements pause until it is restored — and no participant loses housing, cleaning, connectivity, pet care, or stipend while it is fixed.**

### 1.4 Why 12

- **The first cohort gates all others.** Nothing scales on a plan; it scales on a demonstrated onboarding. Cohort 1 has to prove that payments land, that the error controls hold, and that retention and participant-confirmation are real numbers — not intentions.
- **Each cohort proves the model for the next.** A cohort in a different pocket tests a different mix of housing stock, transit access, partner coverage, and neighborhood relationship. Twelve pockets are twelve replications of one model, which is what "replicable city infrastructure" actually requires as evidence.
- **The wildflower framing (owner's words):** starter seeds, planted together in the same season, each one its own plant. Twelve seeds at 5–25 participants each is **60–300 participants** — a framework estimate — which is large enough to mean something and small enough that every person is known.
- **Never all twelve at once.** Deployment is seed-by-seed and wave-by-wave, each wave gated on its own honest report (§2).

### 2.2 Pocket-selection principles (City of San Diego only)

No pocket is chosen yet. These are the principles the site walks will apply.

1. **Jurisdiction: City of San Diego, verified per address.** The 12 seeds are city seeds. A National City or Chula Vista address is out of scope for this plan.
2. **Transit first.** Within a walkable distance of a high-frequency MTS bus or trolley stop (threshold set on the site walk, not from a desk). PRONTO LIFE enrollment support is delivered on site; zero-entropy travel is a pillar, not a perk.
3. **Inside the Navigator's existing footprint.** Each pocket must have, reachable without a car: a food distribution, an FQHC or sliding-scale clinic, a hygiene/showers site, and a pet food pantry or street-vet clinic. Our Navigator already maps 17 core resources plus 12 wellness-animal resources with source and check date on every entry (checked 2026-09-04/05) — pocket selection is a search against that dataset.
4. **Housing that can be made pet-friendly and autism-accommodating.** Verified San Diego moderate rehab runs ~$50–$75/sq ft, so a modest 1,000-sq-ft rehab is ~$50K–$75K minimum. The quadrupled CapEx envelope ($38,000–$114,000 per unit) genuinely covers this; pre-quad figures did not (§4).
5. **One pocket, one cohort per wave.** No pocket carries two cohorts in the same wave — that keeps each pocket a clean test and avoids visible concentration.
6. **Quiet-and-reachable balance.** Sensory considerations count: predictable lighting is achievable, traffic and nightlife noise are flags rather than automatic exclusions, and decompression space exists inside the unit spec (not leftover room).
7. **Neighborhood consent posture.** The community liaison makes first contact with local groups and the district office **before any unit is signed**. No covert placement. No surprise cohort. Ever.
8. **No pocket is announced until units are secured** and no pocket opens with fewer than its minimum cohort size available.
9. **Spread across the city.** Aim for the 12 pockets to cover 8–9 of the city's districts rather than concentrating. Downtown has the densest resource base (day-center meals, shower sites, co-located clinic and housing services) and should hold at most a small share of cohorts, not a majority.

### 2.3 Gate criteria between waves

Metrics are set with partners in advance and **reported whether or not targets are met**. A miss is published; it is never quietly absorbed.

| Gate metric | Proposed bar (to be set with partners before Wave 1 launches) | Where the bar comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Retention at 12 months** | ≥80% of participants continuously housed and still enrolled | Housing First retention is commonly cited at 73–88% (Pathways 88% at 5 years; Finland ~80%; systematic review 2.46× more likely stably housed at 18–24 months) |
| **Payment accuracy** | Disbursement-error rate <2% for two consecutive quarters, and 100% of errors resolved within the published SLA | Our own graduation trigger in the entity/efficiency spec |
| **Participant-confirmation rate** | ≥95% of disbursements confirmed by the participant through their chosen channel within 48 hours | Error control #3 |
| **Grievance SLA** | 100% acknowledged within 24 hours; ≥95% resolved or escalated within 5 business days | Error control #6 |
| **Unit-cost reconciliation** | Actual per-unit CapEx and monthly OpEx published against the framework bands; variances explained | Funding framework Phase 1 gate |
| **Legal/tax** | No unresolved issues on the grant vehicle; counsel's sign-off on record | Funding framework Phase 1 gate |
| **Participant feedback** | ≥2 monthly feedback sessions per cohort held; every resulting change published with attribution | §7 |
| **Animal welfare outcomes** | Recorded for every animal: vet status, pantry access, any foster episode with reunion outcome | Funding framework Phase 1 gate (explicitly includes animals) |
| **Governance** | Cohort council seated and voting on the §7 decisions | §7 |

**Failure handling, stated plainly:**
- A wave does not advance until its gate is met **or** the miss is published with the reason and the corrective action.
- **A gate miss never de-funds anyone.** Cohorts already open keep every right and every line in §4 while the gate is worked. We do not harm people to satisfy a metric.
- If a non-negotiable error control lapses, new disbursements pause and are restored the moment the control is back — with the pause and the fix both reported.

- No re-qualification, no paperwork burden on the participant, no loss of any base line, no probation, no "we'll review in 90 days."
- A participant in the working model may step out and return as often as they choose; a participant in the non-working model may step in for one session, one course, or one season and step back out.
- **Which model a participant is in is not a status that travels.** It is not a performance record, is not shared with anyone outside the case-owner, and is not stored beyond what the individual opts into (data minimization — §6).
- **Non-participation is autonomy, never a deficiency**, and never "soon to be cut." No program staff member has the authority to move a participant between models, reduce a line, or attach a condition. Only the participant moves themselves.

### 4.3 The regenerative-income pathways in detail (working model)

From the Pierce framework as adapted for a participant starting from zero:

- **Skill assessment** — a participant-authored inventory of skills, interests, and what they want to build. Strengths-based; no deficit scoring, no grade, no file that follows them.
- **Gamified training** — opt-in levels with badges and visible progress, built so that stopping is never a loss and a level can always be re-entered. Progress is celebrated; it is never required.
- **Earn-to-learn stipends** — paid *while* training, so learning is affordable in time as well as money. Paid on the same published calendar as everything else, with the same 48-hour confirmation.
- **Micro-enterprise seed support** — start-up support for participant-run work (creative services, culinary, growing, repair, care work, local trades), with the equipment provided rather than deducted and a simple gift-acknowledgment form reviewed by counsel.
- **Digital-economy access** — **equity is access, not just an invitation.** Hardware and connectivity are provided: high-spec laptop, smartphone, tablet, noise-canceling headphones, software and training budget, all as gifted equipment, never deducted from stipends, and **identical in the non-working model** because connectivity is a right, not a work tool.
- **Income targets (framework estimates — quadrupled):** lean ≈ $26,000+/mo against lean outgo ≈ $16,800/mo; premium ≈ $40,000+/mo against premium outgo ≈ $27,600/mo. Regenerative income is designed to **exceed** outgo so each participant builds their own rolling cushion — **lagniappe**: every need covered, plus a bit extra, plus help whenever it is needed.
- **Where the surplus goes:** the participant's own cushion **first**. Only then, voluntarily and downstream, does surplus feed the eight Regenerative Commons channels (education; healthcare facilities and prevention research; environmental conservation; community infrastructure; arts and culture; local economic development; community-led projects; regenerative R&D). Commons flow is vision, not commitment — and never assumed in anyone's budget math.
- **The income-floor layer:** the funding framework carries an income floor / regenerative-income seed layer of $100,000–$200,000 per participant inside the $1M+ envelope (framework estimate) — that is what bridges a participant's own income during the first year. It exists **so that the non-working model is genuinely free to be non-working.**

### 4.4 What the evidence supports, and what it does not

**Supported:** unconditional cash does not discourage work. Stockton SEED (125 residents, $500/month, 24 months, no work requirements, 2019–2021) saw full-time employment rise from 28% to 40%, month-to-month income volatility fall to 19% vs 26% for controls, and Kessler-10 distress move into the "likely well" range in year one. GiveDirectly's Kenya experiment (295 villages, ~$22.50/month arms and ~$500 lump sums) found businesses +14%, revenues +41%, net revenues +52%, **labor supply did not fall**, and mental health improved *more* in the monthly-stream arms. A meta-analysis of 114 cash-transfer studies across 72 programs found strong positive effects on 10 of 13 outcomes and **no dependency effect**. Housing First keeping people housed is the strongest, most-replicated result in the field: 88% retention over five years at Pathways NY vs 47% traditional; 73% of days stably housed across five Canadian cities vs 32%; 2.46× more likely stably housed at 18–24 months in a four-RCT review. Finland's national policy cut long-term homelessness 35% from 2008 to 2015 and 68% from 2008 to 2022, saving society at least €15,000 per housed person per year (2011 estimate).

**Not supported, stated honestly:** the final peer-reviewed Stockton analysis did **not** find the employment gain statistically significant; benefits faded during COVID and after payments ended; housing alone does **not** reliably improve mental health, substance use, or employment — our housing + income combination is a design hypothesis whose components are each backed, not a proven package. Mexico-scale vs million-scale: **no study tests grants at our scale.** None of it proves anything about San Diego specifically.

## 5. ZERO-BARRIER UI ONBOARDING

The owner's framework, honored in spirit and in mechanics.

**1. Automatic enrollment with opt-out.**
- The **team identifies and enrolls eligible people.** Paperwork is done **by the team, not by the participant** — no forms to figure out alone, no document scavenger hunt, no online portal as the price of entry.
- Enrollment is coordinated through existing referral partners (shelter and housing partners, coordinated-entry pathways where applicable) — people already known to the system are found, not made to re-present themselves.
- **Opt-out is absolute and immediate:** any participant may leave at any time, for any reason or no reason, with no explanation required, no exit interview as a condition, and no effect on anything they have already received. Re-entry is always open.
- Identity and phone verification exist **to protect the participant from a mis-payment, never to gate their eligibility.** The team handles documents; the participant is not the filing clerk of their own survival.

**2. Direct and regular disbursement.**
- **Monthly is the default; weekly is available** if a participant prefers it. The cadence belongs to the participant.
- **One primary disbursement rail** for all routine payments, with exceptions documented and minimized (error control #7). Rail changes happen only at phase boundaries.
- **A published payment calendar** with fixed dates shared with participants and the sponsor in advance; any change announced at least 7 days ahead. Calendar adherence is reported as a metric.
- **A test payment confirmed received before any first full payment** (error control #2) — wrong account numbers are discovered at $1, not at full value.
- **Every disbursement is confirmed by the participant within 48 hours** through their chosen channel — call, text, or in person (error control #3). Confirmation is a service to the participant, never an obligation that can trigger a penalty.
- **One published grievance channel** — "my payment is wrong or missing" — acknowledged within 24 hours, resolved or escalated within 5 business days, with fix authority pre-delegated so a case-owner can re-issue without a committee (error control #6).

**3. Optional, non-judgmental financial literacy and peer mentoring.**
- Financial-literacy support and peer mentoring are **offered, never assigned**, and never a condition of anything.
- **Nothing about how a participant spends their money is collected, scored, or asked about.** No budgeting requirement, no receipts to submit for personal spending, no "money-management readiness" gate on anyone's stipend or their standing in the program.
- Peer mentors are paid participants, chosen by the cohort, with a structural firewall: they can support, never decide (§3).

## 6. SACRED PROTECTIONS

These are unconditional, non-negotiable program rules. They are not policies that flex with funding pressure, press interest, evaluation needs, or convenience. **Every staff member and every partner signs this code before the first day and again annually. A partner who violates it loses the partnership.** Anyone may raise a breach through the single grievance channel, and it is escalated directly to the sponsor's compliance owner, bypassing the person named.

### 6.1 SACRED PRIVACY
- **Zero surveillance of persons, families, or spiritual and religious life.** No monitoring of where a participant goes, who visits, what they believe, whether they attend services or meetings, or how they spend their time.
- **Data minimization:** nothing is stored beyond what the individual opts into. Payment and enrollment records exist because money must move; case notes are administrative, not personal; **no case file is a biography.** Records are reviewed quarterly and deleted by default.
- **Participant stories never become marketing content.** No personal narrative, image, or detail appears anywhere public without a separate, specific, revocable written consent for that specific use — and refusing costs nothing, ever.
- **No location tracking, no guest logging, no "wellness checks" as compliance.** A welfare concern is a human conversation, never a surveillance function.

### 6.2 NO PROFIT FROM INJURY OR PASSING
- **No monetization of any person's injury, death, or story — by staff, partners, or media, ever.** No fundraising appeal built on someone's worst day; no "before and after" imagery sold or licensed; no paid speaking, consulting, conference, or content revenue generated from a participant's hardship.
- **Celebration of contributions never becomes exploitation.** Celebrating what a participant built is consented, participant-authored, and consent-revocable. It is never a fundraising asset and never a debt of gratitude.
- **When a participant is hurt or dies, the program's public duty is dignity and silence, not narrative.** No statement, image, or case study is published from it. If the family asks for something said publicly, the family writes it.

### 6.3 NO LEVERAGING FAMILY
- **No member's support is ever contingent on another member's behavior.** No participant's housing, stipend, cleaning, connectivity, or pet care is tied to what a partner, child, parent, sibling, or friend does or does not do.
- **Reunification is by choice only.** There is no family-reunification requirement, no "earn your family back" pathway, and no reporting on family members' conduct. Family participation is invited, never required, and never the price of a home.
- **No member is used as leverage in any direction** — not to encourage behavior, not to secure compliance, not to demonstrate outcomes.

### 6.4 NO CAREER-BUILDING OFF HARM
- **No one builds a career, a credential, a portfolio, a thesis, a book, a consultancy, or a reputation off a participant's harm.** This binds staff, contractors, partners, evaluators, and volunteers identically.
- **Research and evaluation require participant consent, participant compensation, and separate governance approval** — and the participant sees anything written about them before it is filed.
- **Participant-facing roles are paid roles.** No unpaid "experience," no internships that trade dignity for a line on a résumé.

### 6.5 How these protections are held
- They are **not votable down** — not by the cohort council, not by a funder's preference, not by a program lead. Only a participant-governance vote plus owner ratification may ever amend them.
- **Every disbursement pause, wave gate, and audit** operates without ever touching a participant's housing or rights.
- Breach consequences are structural, not personal: partnership termination, removal from participant-facing work, and a published (non-identifying) correction in the monthly error/fix report.

## 7. REINTEGRATION PRINCIPLES AS OPERATING RULES

| Principle | Concrete operating rule |
|---|---|
| **Co-design seats** | Every participant in a cohort is a **voting member of the cohort council**. The council votes on: shared-space rules, the activities calendar, staff hiring input, how contributions are celebrated, the feedback agenda, and how the commons is used locally. Reserved to law/owner: financial and legal compliance, safety obligations, and the §6 protections themselves. In Wave 2 and beyond, two elected participant seats carry votes onto the program board as it forms. |
| **Voluntary participation everywhere** | Every activity, session, training, celebration, and meeting is opt-in. **No attendance tracking that affects anything.** The only thing that is not optional is the money arriving on the calendar. |
| **Dedicated community liaisons** | One per cohort (§3), with reach into the neighborhood as well as the cohort, so the cohort is a neighbor, not an installation. |
| **Shared spaces and activities on equal terms** | Participants, staff, and neighbors use shared space as equals. No separate participant entrance, no staff-only room except where law requires one, no two-tier event. |
| **Public celebration of contributions** | Celebrations are participant-authored and consent-based, and they name **contributions** — skills, work, care, neighborliness — never needs. What gets noticed is what people built. |
| **Continuous feedback loops** | Monthly participant feedback sessions, held at participant-chosen times with food, transport, and pet care covered. **Every resulting change is published with attribution** ("this change came from participant feedback on [date]"), and a standing log shows each item's status. |
| **Adaptive cadence** | **Quarterly policy review** of everything in this document, including the two-model design and the error controls. No policy change affecting participants takes effect without 14 days' notice, except where law requires otherwise — and then the change is explained in plain language. |
| **Error transparency** | A monthly error/fix report published to the cohort, **including months with zero errors** — silence is not a metric. |

## 8. HONEST OPEN QUESTIONS

What this document does **not** yet answer.

1. **Exact pocket selection.** No pocket is chosen. It needs site walks: transit and resource proximity verified on foot, housing stock and landlord willingness tested, rehab feasibility and per-unit cost verification, noise and sensory assessment, and neighborhood first contact. Until then, pocket principles (§2.2) are a method, not a result.
2. **Legal per-cohort structure under fiscal sponsorship.** Whether each cohort is a project under one Model A sponsorship agreement, how participant grants and stipends flow per cohort, gift-acknowledgment forms, and the tax treatment of each payment type all require counsel. The displacement-prioritization criteria — present-tense relief with documented 20+ year displacement as a priority tier, race-neutral — must be shaped by counsel before any money moves.
3. **Sponsor commitments are zero.** No sponsor has been engaged and no sponsor fee is verified per candidate. The 5–15% administrative band is a benchmark from published explainers (verified 2026-09-07), not a rate we hold.
4. **Staff payroll is not costed.** No wage, benefit, or employer-tax figure exists for the four cohort roles. This is a real gap between the participant-side framework and a launchable budget, and it must be closed with the sponsor before Wave 1.
5. **Cohort size per pocket is unresolved** (5, 25, or in between). It drives staffing ratios, CapEx, and how many cohorts a single liaison can honestly hold a relationship with.
6. **The ≤15 caseload cap implies 1–2 navigators per cohort.** How coverage, leave, and after-hours support actually work at a 25-person cohort is not yet designed.
7. **Measurement design is not final.** Target bars are proposed, not agreed with partners; no independent evaluator is engaged; the participant-confirmation baseline is unknown because no payment has ever run.
8. **Housing vehicle undecided per pocket** — lease, master lease, or acquisition. Acquisition fits State capital programs (jurisdiction-level, Phase 2/3 timing); it does not fit Phase 1 per-participant support.
9. **Pet demand is unknown.** Budget lines are ready; how many participants arrive with animals, and of what size and need, is not known. The bands flex by breed, size, and need — the actual spend will land inside them or the bands get revised honestly.
10. **Benefit interaction (SSI/SSDI and similar) is unresolved.** The program's rule is that program support is never reduced because of outside income, and no benefits question is ever solved by cutting someone's support. How that interacts with means-tested benefit rules, and whether a participant's benefits are affected, is a benefits-counsel question we owe every participant an answer to.
11. **Non-working-model durability under funder pressure.** A future funder asking for "workforce outcomes" must not be able to convert the non-working model into a work requirement. That requires a written refusal posture in every funding agreement — not yet drafted.
12. **Vehicles, staff tooling, and consultant bundle items** in the source framework are **not** in scope of this document. Vehicles are treated as out-of-scope corporate assets unless the owner directs otherwise; participant-side equipment (laptop, phone, tablet, headphones) is in scope as gifted equipment. *(Amended by Amendment B §12.1: this is no longer accurate for vehicles — see item 16.)*
13. **"Backpay" wording remains a legal question, not a copy question.** The program language used publicly must be counsel-approved.
14. **Internal consistency to reconcile:** the published Seed page describes Phase 1 as all 12 cohorts launching, while this operating plan deploys them in three gated waves (1–2 → 3–6 → 7–12). The waves are the operating reality; the public wording should be aligned before the next publish.
15. **No figures are committed.** Every number here is a framework estimate — quadrupled — pending real quotes, sponsor costing, and counsel review.
16. **Vehicles are no longer out of scope (Amendment B — OWNER DECISION RESERVED).** A movement capability cannot be delivered without vehicles, and a safety transfer cannot be delivered without an *unbranded* one. Amendment B therefore makes vehicles a real, costed line and **reserves the choice to the owner: lease, own, or rent per pocket**, with insurance, driver licensing (including whether a paid participant crew member may drive a program vehicle), and the branding constraint addressed before any vehicle is acquired. **The participant side does not become a vehicle-gifting program by this amendment.**
