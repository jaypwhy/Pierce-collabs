## 1. THE MODULAR SYSTEM — module cards

A **module** is a legal container with one job. It holds what its job requires and it is **forbidden** from holding anything else — that second half of the sentence is the whole design. Each card declares six things: what it **holds**, what it must **never hold**, which **bridges** it uses, its **status**, its **severability**, and what **counsel** must validate.

**Six cards, six statuses, and the honest count: one module is decided (the core), one is a bridge-phase option, three are optional, one is not-yet.**

**On complexity, because it is the owner's own stated concern.** Filing one entity is one set of books, one chart of accounts, one set of filings, one place a payment error can hide. Every module added multiplies that. **That is the argument for this being a design and not a plan:** the value of the card format is that a module can be declined on the strength of one card, before it costs anything. The two rules that make that true are severability and plug-and-function (§3 b, c).

- **STATUS: CORE** — the structure the owner decided (2026-10-07). **Not formed.**
- **HOLDS:** the mission (housing, health, food & hygiene, regenerative-income navigation, zero-entropy travel); **donations**; **participant support grants** under the verified mechanism — **Rev. Rul. 2003-12**, 501(c)(3) grants to distressed individuals from "detached and disinterested generosity," excluded from recipients' gross income under IRC §102 (**checked 2026-09-07**; caveat travels with it: the ruling's facts are disaster-relief grants, and generalizing to ongoing phased grants at our scale is counsel's call); the **Future Fund**; the **sacred protections** (DOC 1 §6); program governance.
- **NEVER HOLDS:** commercial operations; private benefit; any insider's interest in the flow of charitable dollars.
- **BRIDGES USED:** B1 (out), B2 (out), B3 (out), B4 (both ways), B6 (in), B7 (both ways), B8 (both ways).
- **SEVERABILITY:** **Not severable — it is the core.** Every other module must be removable without collapsing it (§3 rule b).
- **COUNSEL VALIDATION:** formation and articles; the mission and purpose clauses; the individual-grant program's **open charitable class and objective criteria**; the displacement-prioritization tier; the Future Fund vesting schedule; every charter element in §5.

### MODULE 2 — FISCAL SPONSOR (BRIDGE MODULE)

## 2. THE CONNECTION POINTS — the bridges

Each bridge is a **named interface**: what moves, the **legal valve** that makes it legitimate, the **paperwork** that documents it, **who validates** it, and the **transparency note** that can be published in plain language, counsel-approved. Eight bridges. **Any arrow between modules that is not one of these is not a connection — it is an exposure.**

### B1 — CHARITABLE GRANT (program purpose)

- **Direction:** core → an exempt or governmental recipient (a partner, a project, an affiliate's charitable program).
- **Valve:** a grant for a charitable purpose, with documented selection criteria and intended-use restrictions.
- **Paperwork:** grant agreement; purpose restriction; budget; reporting schedule; for a non-public-charity recipient, expenditure responsibility.
- **Who validates:** counsel on the form and the restrictions; the sponsor during the bridge phase.
- **Transparency:** the funded program, the criteria, and the reporting obligation are publishable; totals by program are publishable; **no donor identity, ever** (§3 rule e).

### B2 — INDIVIDUAL RELIEF GRANT (the participant grant)

- **Direction:** core → an individual participant (or household).
- **Valve:** **the verified mechanism — Rev. Rul. 2003-12** (checked 2026-09-07): grants by a 501(c)(3) to distressed individuals out of "detached and disinterested generosity," excluded from recipients' gross income under IRC §102. Facts caveat: the ruling addresses disaster-relief grants; generalizing to ongoing phased grants at our scale is counsel's call.
- **Paperwork:** an **open, need-based charitable class** with published objective criteria; **present-tense relief**; documented displacement history as a **prioritization tier only, race-neutral** (the Evanston lesson: its race-restricted eligibility drew a federal lawsuit — *Flinn v. Evanston*, May 2024; **outcome not verified, do not cite a resolution**); a gift acknowledgment; benefits counseling alongside every payment.
- **Who validates:** counsel, and only counsel, **before any money moves.** This is the bridge the Wave-1 gate names ("legal vehicle for participant funds documented", DOC 1 §2.1).
- **Transparency:** the criteria and the prioritization tier are publishable; **individual amounts are never published** (sacred privacy, DOC 1 §6.1). The household knows its own number; the public sees the structure.

### B3 — PROGRAM-RELATED INVESTMENT (PRI)

- **Direction:** core → a property or venture module, or → a third party's charitable venture.
- **Valve:** **the PRI rules — a charitable purpose, below-market terms, and no significant profit motive** as an investment.
- **Paperwork:** a written PRI analysis made **at the time of the investment** (not reconstructed later); valuation and terms; monitoring; and the ability to demonstrate the charitable purpose independently of any return.
- **Who validates:** counsel, plus the board's conflict-of-interest process, sharpened where the owner holds an affiliate interest (§5.9).
- **Transparency:** the existence and purpose of the investment are publishable; the counterparty relationship is publishable; commercial terms stay commercially normal.
- **The honest note:** **PRI is the one bridge that lets charitable money legitimately move into a for-profit entity — and it is the most scrutinized bridge in the whole structure.** It is not a pour, it is not automatic, and it is not a substitute for the charitable purpose test.

### B4 — ARM'S-LENGTH SERVICES CONTRACT AT FAIR MARKET VALUE

- **Direction:** both ways (the core buys services from a module; a module buys services from the core).
- **Valve:** a documented fair-market-value basis for the rate.
- **Paperwork:** written agreement with scope, rate, term, and termination; the FMV basis on file; a conflict-of-interest approval record where a related party is involved.
- **Who validates:** counsel (template + related-party process); the board approves related-party contracts disinterestedly.
- **Transparency:** scope and purpose publishable; **rates are the module's business** — publishable if the owner wants, not required.

### B5 — LEASE AT FAIR MARKET VALUE

- **Direction:** property module → the core, or → a participant household directly.
- **Valve:** FMV rent with a documented basis. **A rent intentionally below market is a different instrument** (see B3, and §5.9) and must not be dressed as a lease. This is exactly the distinction the owner's "filter system" question runs into: the honest way to move value cheaply is to name the instrument, not to mislabel the rent.
- **Paperwork:** the lease; the FMV basis; and the **wind-down / resident-protection clause** (§1, Module 3).
- **Who validates:** counsel.
- **Transparency:** **that the program leases at market from a related entity is publishable** — saying so is what makes a structure that looks like self-dealing on the surface readable and defensible underneath.

### B6 — PROFIT UPSTREAMING FROM A CONTROLLED SUBSIDIARY

- **Direction:** venture (or property) → core.
- **Valve:** a distribution or dividend **with the tax accounted for**; a genuinely charitable purpose for the receiving funds; the board evaluating fairness to both sides.
- **Paperwork:** an intercompany policy; transfer-pricing-style documentation if services or IP cross; the paying entity's tax return.
- **Who validates:** counsel + the auditor.
- **Transparency:** the existence of upstreaming is publishable; the amount is publishable at the entity level. **This bridge is where the structure's credibility lives or dies.** A structure that only ever flows outward is a funding mechanism. A structure that flows back is a business with a charitable shareholder. **A structure with no documented valve either way is the red flag.**

### B7 — SHARED-SERVICES AGREEMENT

- **Direction:** sponsor → project (bridge phase), or core ↔ modules (later).
- **Valve:** an allocation of **real cost** on a defensible basis (headcount, transaction count, revenue, or a documented cost study). **Never a profit center.**
- **Paperwork:** written agreement; the cost-allocation methodology; the fee schedule; and, in the bridge phase, the **disbursement SLA** (requests in by X → funds out by Y) — the single most error-relevant clause in the whole Phase-0 paper set.
- **Who validates:** counsel, and the sponsor's finance lead during the bridge phase.
- **Transparency:** the fee, its basis, and the SLA are publishable. Our donor-facing posture is that an administrative percentage **is stated, not hidden.**

### B8 — LICENSE / IP AGREEMENT

- **Direction:** any module ↔ any module — the name, the marks, the curriculum, any platform, published content.
- **Valve:** a written license at FMV, **or a deliberate no-charge license** where charitable purpose is served. The difference must be on paper; the paper is what makes the choice legitimate.
- **Paperwork:** license; scope; quality control; exclusivity (probably none); termination.
- **Who validates:** counsel — and the **owner**, because the entity name and the marks are reserved (§7.2).
- **Transparency:** publishable in principle; whether brand or name terms ever publish is the owner's call.

### 2.1 WHAT IS NOT A VALVE — the red-flag list

- **A "free pour" toward any private interest** — money moving between modules with no written instrument, no basis, and no paper. This is the owner's own phrase being answered honestly: it is the one thing the architecture must be provably unable to do.
- **Any transfer to or for the benefit of a founder, officer, director, or their family** that is not an arm's-length, documented, disinterestedly approved transaction — **private benefit, inurement, and §4958 excess-benefit territory.**
- **Below-market use of charitable assets dressed up as a "lease" or a "contract."**
- **Undirected intercompany movement with no policy** — the classic failure mode of a founder-built multi-entity structure.
- **A donation accepted into a for-profit module and receipted as deductible.** There is no exception, no routing, and no "we'll sort it later."
- **One module guaranteeing another's debts** in either direction without documented analysis (charitable assets collateralizing a commercial obligation is its own counsel question, and the answer is usually no).
- **Any module whose failure would take a home away.** If test (b) in §3 fails, the module is not built — regardless of what it would have earned.

## 3. PLUG-AND-FUNCTION RULES (design law)

These are the rules any version of this structure has to satisfy. They are **design law for the team**; they bind nothing until the owner adopts them and counsel reads them.

**(a) No connection exists without a written valve.** The owner's word was "pour"; we keep the owner's word and write the constraint beside it. What moves between modules moves through a documented instrument — a grant, an investment, a contract, a lease, a distribution, an allocation, a license — **or it does not move.** A free pour toward a private interest is the red flag: that is private benefit, inurement, and §4958 excess-benefit exposure, and the exposure falls on the person who benefited, not just the entity.

**(b) Every bridge is severable.** Removing a module must not collapse the core. The test, applied to each card in §1: **unplug this module — does any participant lose housing, a stipend, a RIGHT, or a protected line?** If yes, the design fails and is revised. No module earns its place by being load-bearing for a home.

**(c) Adding a module later must not require re-architecting the core.** The test: the core's bylaws, budget structure, and program rules should not have to change to accept a new plug. If accepting a module forces a charter amendment, it was not a plug. **Known, planned exception:** the Advocacy module may require one clause about grants to a non-charitable-purpose affiliate. That is an anticipated amendment, not a surprise, and it is named so it cannot arrive as one.

**(d) Donations land only in the core — or in a Model A sponsor.** **Donations into any for-profit module are not deductible, and donors are told so plainly, before the gift, in writing.** No receipt is issued, no exception is made, and no gift is routed to keep a deduction alive. If a donor wants a deduction, the gift goes to the core.

**(e) TRANSPARENCY OF STRUCTURE, PRIVACY OF DONORS.** The structure diagram and every bridge's purpose are **publishable in plain language, counsel-approved**, on our own pages — not as marketing, but because a reader should be able to see that every dollar's path between modules has an instrument, a basis, and a name. **Donor identities stay private** (philanthropic-privacy directive, DOC 1 §6.1). **The structure is public; the people are not.**

### 4.1 PATH C deserves its own paragraph, because it is the owner's question

The owner asked whether a parent with a nonprofit, a for-profit, and donations could work as a "filter system." **Honest answer: the modules are all legitimate, and the control relationship is exactly the part we must not decide.** Two shapes exist in ordinary practice — the **charity owning the for-profit subsidiary**, and the **owner holding both as siblings** — and each carries its own tax, UBIT, and private-benefit analysis. **Which one, whether either, and whether the core can be a "parent" in the corporate-control sense at all, is a single counsel question.** The structure question, restated in one line: *which container is on top is the question the architecture exists to defer, not to answer.*

### 4.2 Formation steps, as module activation

1. **Incorporate the core** — articles filed in the chosen state, with the purpose clause and the charter elements in §5.
2. **Bylaws and the four policies** — conflict of interest, whistleblower, document retention, compensation (the same four the graduation triggers name).
3. **EIN, then the bank account** — one primary operating account; the bridge phase's single disbursement rail (error control #7), with any exception documented and dual-approved.
4. **IRS determination — the 1023-vs-1023-EZ judgment.**
   - **1023-EZ** is the short form, available only to organizations meeting the IRS's published eligibility criteria (its own eligibility worksheet — **a process fact, not re-verified in this session; verify before application**).
   - **Our profile reasoning** (reasoning, not a decision): we intend to raise substantial sums, make **grants to individuals**, operate across more than a neighborhood over time, and possibly hold interests in for-profit modules. **Each of those is a reason counsel may read the short form as unavailable or as the wrong instrument for us.** A short-form filing is also a harder record to build a structure around afterwards.
   - **The operator's lean is therefore toward the full 1023 with the structure described in it** — but the eligibility call belongs to **counsel**, on the facts at filing time, and it sits on the counsel question list (§5.10).
   - **The honest cost of choosing wrong:** a rejected or defective application burns a filing cycle, and the application fee is not automatically refunded or transferred. That is the price of the judgment, and it is why it is counsel's.
   - **Under PATH B this step does not block operation** — the sponsor carries the program while the application is pending. That is the glide's entire purpose.

## 5. BYLAWS DESIGN POINTS (charter elements counsel validates)

*What the founding documents must contain for this program to be what it says it is. Design points, not drafted language.*

**5.1 Philanthropic privacy as a charter element.** Donors are never named, listed, or implied. No donor-branded program, no sponsorship branding anywhere. This is a **bylaw-level** commitment, not a communications policy, so it survives a future board with different instincts.

**5.2 The refusal posture (DOC 1 §8.11).** The non-working model must not be convertible into a work requirement by a funder's preference. Written **both** as a charter clause **and** as a standard clause in every funding agreement — DOC 1's own wording is that this is "not yet drafted," and this point is where it gets drafted.

**5.3 Sacred protections as charter-level elements (DOC 1 §6).** The five protections named whole; the **"not votable down"** rule; the amendment path (participant-governance vote **plus** owner ratification); and the escalation route that lets anyone raise a breach **bypassing the person named** (DOC 1 §6 opening).

**5.4 Need-not-merit and opt-out as charter elements.** Eligibility and standing defined by **need, never merit**; every activity, session, training, and meeting **opt-in**, with no attendance tracking that affects anything; the only non-optional thing is **money arriving on the calendar.**

**5.5 The participant-funds legal vehicle (DOC 1 §2.1, the Wave-1 gate criterion).** The bylaws must not conflict with whatever vehicle counsel designs for participant funds — grant, trust, gift acknowledgment, or other. **The same question at household scale is the child sub-envelope vehicle** (DOC 2 §11.5, options A/B/C, option B recommended at a figure the owner sets), and a deceased member's envelope treatment (DOC 2 §12.2).

**5.6 The DV override (DOC 2).** Safety planning is a **non-negotiable override**. The bylaws must permit an **urgent safety transfer without a governance vote**, and must carry the address-protection and no-disclosure rules, including when the program is asked for a location. A safety response that waits for a quorum is not a safety response.

**5.7 The care/education supplies line and the FOOD line (Amendment A, 2026-10-01).** Both stated in the **budget policy** as protected program-funded lines, so a future board cannot quietly reclassify them as discretionary — the same protection logic as the two RIGHTS lines (cleaning, connectivity).

**5.8 Board design.** Size, terms, quorum; the **two elected participant seats with votes as the board forms** (DOC 1 §7); the founder's role (member? officer? neither?); and **term limits as a structural protection** — institutions drift, and limits are the cheapest defense ever invented. Nothing here is pre-decided; all of it is counsel-and-owner.

**5.9 Conflict-of-interest policy, SHARPENED where the owner holds an affiliate interest — this is where the architecture has to bite.** If the owner ever holds a financial interest in a property or venture module, then all five of these, in writing, on every transaction:

1. every transaction with that module is a **related-party transaction** requiring **disinterested** approval and a **documented FMV basis**;
2. **the owner recuses** from that vote;
3. the **§4958 excess-benefit analysis is run and documented on each transaction** — not once at setup;
4. the arrangement is **disclosed in the 990-series filing** wherever the form asks for it;
5. **intermediate sanctions are personal, not corporate** — an excess benefit can be assessed against the person who benefited, so this policy protects the owner as much as it protects the charity.

Alongside it, the three companion policies the graduation triggers name: **whistleblower, document retention, and compensation.**

**5.10 The counsel question list — DOC 1 §8 carried whole, plus this document's additions.** DOC 1's list: per-cohort structure under a sponsor (§8.2); gift acknowledgment and the tax treatment of each payment type (§8.2); the displacement-prioritization criteria (§8.2); benefit interaction, SSI/SSDI and similar (§8.10); public "backpay" wording (§8.13); crew insurance, liability, and mover classification (§8.17). DOC 1's Wave-1 gate: the participant-funds legal vehicle (§2.1). DOC 2's: the mandatory-reporting-versus-privacy boundary at household scale, and the child sub-envelope vehicle (§11.5). **This document's additions:** PRI terms and documentation; the related-party transaction process; profit upstreaming and its tax treatment; **whether a participant may be employed by, contract with, or own part of a venture module**; the 1023-versus-1023-EZ eligibility call; the control relationship between the core and any for-profit module (the parent question, §4.1); and whether an advocacy module is needed at all.

## 9. Quotable close

**The structure, for a person.** Somebody who needs a home does not care which entity holds the lease. What they care about is that the money arrives on the calendar, that nobody can take the home back to balance a spreadsheet, and that a form they filled out wrong on a bad day never becomes a reason to lose it. That is the test every sentence above has to pass, and it is harder than it looks — **every module we add is another set of books, another filing, and another place a payment can go wrong.**

So the rules we hold are the owner's rules, written down where they can be checked: **transparent structure, private donors**; **no arrow without a valve**; **no module that cannot be unplugged**; and **no home that depends on a business decision.** The owner asked how to add bridges and connection points to a structure that can flex — and the honest answer is that a flexible structure is not one with more ways to pour money around. It is one where every path has a name, a piece of paper, a validator, and a plain-language explanation a stranger could read.

**Nothing in this document exists yet.** There is no entity, no counsel, no sponsor, no name, and no signature — and that is not a gap in the plan. It is the plan's first and most important feature. **Everyone has a place to call home.**
