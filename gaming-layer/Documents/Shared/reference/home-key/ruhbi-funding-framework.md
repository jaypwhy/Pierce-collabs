## 5a. ENTITY STRUCTURE - LEAD RECOMMENDATION (2026-09-06, pending owner confirmation + counsel validation)
**Recommendation: two-layer phased structure - start under fiscal sponsorship, graduate to an independent 501(c)(3) public charity; keep a separate LLC for any future earned-income ventures.**
- Layer 1 (Phase 0, fastest): operate the seed program under an established fiscal sponsor (existing 501(c)(3)) - sponsor handles compliance and donation processing while our own IRS determination is pending. Speed, credibility, low overhead; matches the gated ladder.
- Layer 2 (parallel): incorporate an independent 501(c)(3) public charity with a mission-aligned board; migrate when determination lands. Public-charity status (not private foundation) keeps donor incentives strong and self-dealing rules light.
- The Future Fund participant grants run as a published, criteria-based charitable relief program (open class, objective criteria) - the structure Stockton SEED used to put cash in individuals' hands legally; precedent research being gathered to back this with citations.
- A future LLC (or subsidiary) holds any revenue-generating activity (Creative Studio services, etc.) to keep UBIT off the charity.
- NOT recommended: operating as a private foundation (heavier tax + self-dealing rules if the founder is the dominant funder), or an LLC/L3C as the main vehicle (donations not deductible).
- Required: tax attorney validates the whole structure, the $1M+ grant vesting schedule, and the displacement-prioritization criteria before any money moves. This is an operator's recommendation, not legal advice.

## 5b. BACKPAY FOR DISPLACEMENT - HONEST FRAMING (2026-09-06)
- What a charity CAN do: relieve present need with priority criteria; provide maximum-padding tiers for documented long-ago displacement; cover settlement-in costs that implicitly acknowledge compounded loss.
- What it generally CANNOT do: pay literal retroactive compensation ("backpay") for past harm - that is a legal claim, not a charitable purpose; counsel must shape the program language.
- Precedent to study: Evanston, IL Restorative Housing Program (first US local reparations-for-housing program, 2021) - present-tense housing grants with historical-prioritization criteria; researcher verifying details and structure for our use.

## 1. THE PHASED LADDER

Per-participant building blocks (framework estimates, quadrupled): move-in CapEx **$38K–$114K** one-time; fixed OpEx **$10.8K–$22.8K/mo** (includes cleaning and connectivity as RIGHTS); variable stipends **$3.8K–$7.6K/mo** (includes pet care $600–$1,200/mo); combined monthly support **$14.6K–$30.4K/mo ≈ $175K–$365K/yr**; Future Fund grant **$1M+ per participant** (full-vision figure, phased near-term).

### Phase 0 — Foundation (framework estimate: $200K–$500K small ops budget, new estimate created for this framework)
- **Participants:** 0 (build before housing anyone).
- **What it funds:** entity formation; legal/tax counsel for the grant structure (the $1M+ grant needs a vetted vehicle — gift, trust, or grantor arrangement — before any money moves); first unit secured and renovated (one Tier-1 CapEx $38K–$57K); partner agreements (shelter, food, health, transit, animal-welfare).
- **Gate to Phase 1:** legal vehicle for participant funds documented; one unit move-in ready to the accommodation standards (autism + animal specs); at least two operating partners signed; Phase 1 capital identified (committed pledges with schedule count, labeled as such).

### Phase 1 — First Cohort: 5–10 participants (framework estimate)
- **Capital required, first year:** ~**$6M–$15M** = CapEx $190K–$1.14M (5×$38K to 10×$114K) + first-year support $0.9M–$3.6M (5–10 × $175K–$365K) + Future Fund grant commitments $5M–$10M+ ($1M+ × headcount, phased disbursement — see §3).
- **Gate to Phase 2:** 12-month retention and stability tracked honestly (target bar set with partners in advance, reported whether or not met); per-unit actual costs reconciled against framework bands; no unresolved legal/tax issues on the grant vehicle; participant feedback (including animals' welfare outcomes) formally collected.

### Phase 2 — Demonstration: 25–50 participants (framework estimate)
- **Capital required:** ~**$30M–$75M** = CapEx $0.95M–$5.7M + annual support $4.4M–$18.2M + grant commitments $25M–$50M+.
- **Gate to Phase 3:** multi-cohort retention data across at least 2 years; unit economics proven inside (or honestly revised from) the bands; at least one durable non-philanthropic funding stream secured; independent evaluation partner engaged.

### Phase 3 — Full Vision: 100+ participants (framework estimate)
- **Capital required:** **$100M+** (100 × $1M+ grant envelope, plus CapEx $3.8M–$11.4M and annual support $17.5M–$36.5M inside long-run operations).
- **Gate (ongoing):** the model replicates — second-site or second-cohort launch using this ladder; public norm case published with real (not projected) numbers.

## 2. FUNDING SOURCES MENU

*Generic categories only. No funding source is secured except the founder-directed philanthropic structure described in the owner's own terms (§2a). Nothing here promises outcomes.*

**(a) Philanthropic guaranteed-income funding** — privately and independently secured by the founder; specifics not disclosed, for donor privacy and protection. What must be disclosed will be disclosed. Individuals are encouraged to research philanthropy independently and may open philanthropy to additional resources. Treat as founder-directed structure, not a documented figure; do not claim verified amounts. Fit: the natural home for the Future Fund grants and Phase 0/1 risk capital — philanthropy funded Stockton SEED's entire ~$3M pilot the same way (evidence base §1).

**(b) Government programs.**
- *HUD Continuum of Care (CoC):* HUD's competitive homelessness-assistance program funding permanent supportive housing, rapid rehousing, and services — a June 1, 2026 NOFO announced $4.04B for the CoC program. Funds flow through local CoC bodies (in San Diego, the regional CoC) — competitive, compliance-heavy, renewal-based. Fit: Phase 2+ operating support and services match; poor fit for Phase 0/1 speed.
- *California's Project Homekey (name echo — verified 2026-09-06 what it actually funds):* State HCD grant funding to local jurisdictions to **acquire, rehabilitate, and/or construct** capital assets — hotels, commercial and multifamily buildings, modular housing — to create units for people experiencing homelessness; created 2020 as a COVID response. Across three rounds HCD awarded ~$3.6–$3.78B for 261 projects / ~15,962 homes; successor Homekey+ ($2.2B from Prop 1) targets permanent supportive housing for veterans and people with behavioral-health challenges. **Name-echo note:** our "Project Home Key" shares the name's spirit but is a different, independent initiative; Homekey funds *jurisdictions acquiring buildings*, not per-person grants — fit is Phase 2/3 capital for acquiring units in partnership with the City/County, not Phase 1 per-participant support. (Search-snippet verification; get a direct hcd.ca.gov link before site publication.)

**(c) Corporate CSR (category only, no company speculation):** typically funds furnishings, connectivity, starter tech bundles, employee-volunteer builds, pilot sponsorships. Fit: in-kind and Tier-1 CapEx offsets in Phase 1; unit-sponsor packages in Phase 2. Never projected as core capital.

**(d) Social impact bonds / pay-for-success (mechanism + verified precedent):** private investors supply up-front capital; government repays (with return) only if pre-agreed outcomes are met — risk sits with investors, not the public. Verified precedent: **Denver Supportive Housing SIB — February 2016: Denver and eight private investors closed an $8.6M investment funding supportive housing for 250 frequent jail-system users, with city outcome payments over five years tied to housing stability and reduced jail days.** Fit: Phase 2+ only — requires measurable outcomes and a government payer, which requires Phase 1 data first.

**(e) Community / mutual-aid fundraising:** grassroots donations, benefit events, recurring small-donor circles. Typically funds flexible gaps (pet emergencies, move-in extras, community-engagement stipends). Fit: all phases as gap filler; never the capital base.

**(f) Participant-generated regenerative surplus reinvestment:** per the master framework, regenerative income exceeds outgo so participants build their own rolling cushion first (lagniappe), with surplus flowing outward to the eight Regenerative Commons channels (vision, not commitment). Fit: Phase 2+ at the earliest — Phase 1 participants' surplus is theirs; any reinvestment is voluntary and downstream, never assumed in Phase 1 math.

## 4. HONESTY SECTION

- **The $1M scale is untested.** The evidence base is explicit: Kenya transfers are ~$22.50/mo and ~$500 lump sums; no study tests grants at $250K+/million scale — "large grants work even better" is a projection and must be labeled as one.
- **The phased ladder is the mitigation.** We never deploy $100M on an untested scale — each phase risks only its own capital and must prove its gate before the next phase unlocks.
- **Nothing here promises outcomes** — to participants, funders, or the public. Targets are set with partners in advance and reported whether or not met.
- **Philanthropic figures are founder-directed structure, not documented totals.** No verified amounts claimed; no donors named, per owner directive on philanthropic privacy (2026-09-06).
- Every projection is labeled; verified market figures underneath the bands are in `sd-cost-verification.md` (checked 2026-09-06): FMR studio $2,062 / 1BR $2,248; utilities ~$290–320; connectivity ~$100–140; cleaning ~$200–500; living wage $30.71/hr; rehab $50–75/sq ft.
