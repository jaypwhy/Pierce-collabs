## 1. COHORT CHARTER

### 1.1 What a seed cohort IS

A **seed cohort** is a small starter group of **5–25 participants** in **one pocket of the City of San Diego**. It is not a program tier, not a waiting room, and not a pilot with an end date. It is one complete delivery of the whole system at human scale, small enough that every participant is a known person to a named team.

Each cohort has exactly five defining parts:

1. **One pocket.** A walkable area of the City of San Diego — never the County, never split across two neighborhoods. The pocket is chosen so that transit, food, health, hygiene, and pet care already exist within reach (see §2.2).
2. **One housing arrangement.** Either a **single housing cluster** (a building or a set of units on one site) or **distributed units** scattered inside the same pocket. The choice is per pocket, driven by what real estate is actually available and pet-friendly.
3. **One case-owner team.** The four named roles in §3, with **exactly one named case-owner attached to each participant's payment file** — no orphan files, no "everyone thought someone else sent it."
4. **The whole six-pillar system at once.** Housing, health, food, hygiene, regenerative income, and zero-entropy travel arrive together, as one navigable system — never as a list of referrals for the participant to work through alone. Wellness animals arrive with their people from day one.
5. **One governance body.** Every participant in the cohort is a voting member of the **cohort council**, seated as soon as cohort 1 opens (§7).

### 1.2 Charter clauses (the rules that define membership)

- **Eligibility is need, not merit.** The cohort enrolls people who are unsheltered or housing-insecure in the City of San Diego. There is no readiness test, no sobriety requirement, no "housing-ready" score, no work requirement in either participation model (§4), and no behavioral condition on housing.
- **Enrollment is opt-out, not opt-in.** The team identifies and enrolls eligible people — **the paperwork is done by the team, not by the participant** (§5).
- **Every participant gets the same base package.** §4 lays out both participation models side by side and they are **identical in every material line**: same housing quality, same cleaning and connectivity rights, same pet setup and pet care, same flexible stipend, same access to wellness-animal accommodations, same autism-accommodation build specs. The difference between the models is _only_ whether regenerative-income pathways are active. It is never a difference in what people receive.
- **Cleaning services and connectivity are RIGHTS.** Permanent budget lines in every cohort and every model — never removed, never reduced, never used as a lever.
- **Wellness animals are requirements, not extras.** Pet-friendly housing is an acquisition, lease, and renovation requirement. Pet setup and monthly pet care are in every cohort budget. Settling-in padding covers people **and** animals.
- **Housing rights do not sunset.** Regeneration is not an exit ramp. There is no date at which a participant is expected to "graduate out" of a home. The program's job is permanent stability, not throughput.
- **Backpay posture.** The Future Fund accommodates people displaced 20+ years ago by circumstances beyond their control, as **present-tense relief for current housing need with documented long-ago displacement as a prioritization tier** — race-neutral, inside an open charitable class. It is not literal retroactive compensation, which a 501(c)(3) generally cannot pay; counsel shapes the criteria before any money moves. The quad-directive headroom, the flexible stipend, settling-in padding, and lagniappe as the default posture are how that shows up in practice.

Non-negotiable rule across all four stages: **if any non-negotiable error control lapses, new disbursements pause until it is restored — and no participant loses housing, cleaning, connectivity, pet care, or stipend while it is fixed.**

### 1.4 Why 12

- **The first cohort gates all others.** Nothing scales on a plan; it scales on a demonstrated onboarding. Cohort 1 has to prove that payments land, that the error controls hold, and that retention and participant-confirmation are real numbers — not intentions.
- **Each cohort proves the model for the next.** A cohort in a different pocket tests a different mix of housing stock, transit access, partner coverage, and neighborhood relationship. Twelve pockets are twelve replications of one model, which is what "replicable city infrastructure" actually requires as evidence.
- **The wildflower framing (owner's words):** starter seeds, planted together in the same season, each one its own plant. Twelve seeds at 5–25 participants each is **60–300 participants** — a framework estimate — which is large enough to mean something and small enough that every person is known.
- **Never all twelve at once.** Deployment is seed-by-seed and wave-by-wave, each wave gated on its own honest report (§2).

### 2.2 Pocket-selection principles (City of San Diego only)

No pocket is chosen yet. These are the principles the site walks will apply.

1. **Jurisdiction: City of San Diego, verified per address.** The 12 seeds are city seeds. A National City or Chula Vista address is out of scope for this plan.
2. **Transit first.** Within a walkable distance of a high-frequency MTS bus or trolley stop (threshold set on the site walk, not from a desk). PRONTO LIFE enrollment support is delivered on site; zero-entropy travel is a pillar, not a perk.
3. **Inside the Navigator's existing footprint.** Each pocket must have, reachable without a car: a food distribution, an FQHC or sliding-scale clinic, a hygiene/showers site, and a pet food pantry or street-vet clinic. Our Navigator already maps 17 core resources plus 12 wellness-animal resources with source and check date on every entry (checked 2026-09-04/05) — pocket selection is a search against that dataset.
4. **Housing that can be made pet-friendly and autism-accommodating.** Verified San Diego moderate rehab runs ~$50–$75/sq ft, so a modest 1,000-sq-ft rehab is ~$50K–$75K minimum. The quadrupled CapEx envelope ($38,000–$114,000 per unit) genuinely covers this; pre-quad figures did not (§4).
5. **One pocket, one cohort per wave.** No pocket carries two cohorts in the same wave — that keeps each pocket a clean test and avoids visible concentration.
6. **Quiet-and-reachable balance.** Sensory considerations count: predictable lighting is achievable, traffic and nightlife noise are flags rather than automatic exclusions, and decompression space exists inside the unit spec (not leftover room).
7. **Neighborhood consent posture.** The community liaison makes first contact with local groups and the district office **before any unit is signed**. No covert placement. No surprise cohort. Ever.
8. **No pocket is announced until units are secured** and no pocket opens with fewer than its minimum cohort size available.
9. **Spread across the city.** Aim for the 12 pockets to cover 8–9 of the city's districts rather than concentrating. Downtown has the densest resource base (day-center meals, shower sites, co-located clinic and housing services) and should hold at most a small share of cohorts, not a majority.

### 2.3 Gate criteria between waves

Metrics are set with partners in advance and **reported whether or not targets are met**. A miss is published; it is never quietly absorbed.

|Gate metric|Proposed bar (to be set with partners before Wave 1 launches)|Where the bar comes from|
|---|---|---|
|**Retention at 12 months**|≥80% of participants continuously housed and still enrolled|Housing First retention is commonly cited at 73–88% (Pathways 88% at 5 years; Finland ~80%; systematic review 2.46× more likely stably housed at 18–24 months)|
|**Payment accuracy**|Disbursement-error rate <2% for two consecutive quarters, and 100% of errors resolved within the published SLA|Our own graduation trigger in the entity/efficiency spec|
|**Participant-confirmation rate**|≥95% of disbursements confirmed by the participant through their chosen channel within 48 hours|Error control #3|
|**Grievance SLA**|100% acknowledged within 24 hours; ≥95% resolved or escalated within 5 business days|Error control #6|
|**Unit-cost reconciliation**|Actual per-unit CapEx and monthly OpEx published against the framework bands; variances explained|Funding framework Phase 1 gate|
|**Legal/tax**|No unresolved issues on the grant vehicle; counsel's sign-off on record|Funding framework Phase 1 gate|
|**Participant feedback**|≥2 monthly feedback sessions per cohort held; every resulting change published with attribution|§7|
|**Animal welfare outcomes**|Recorded for every animal: vet status, pantry access, any foster episode with reunion outcome|Funding framework Phase 1 gate (explicitly includes animals)|
|**Governance**|Cohort council seated and voting on the §7 decisions|§7|

**Failure handling, stated plainly:**

- A wave does not advance until its gate is met **or** the miss is published with the reason and the corrective action.
- **A gate miss never de-funds anyone.** Cohorts already open keep every right and every line in §4 while the gate is worked. We do not harm people to satisfy a metric.
- If a non-negotiable error control lapses, new disbursements pause and are restored the moment the control is back — with the pause and the fix both reported.

### 4.3 The regenerative-income pathways in detail (working model)

From the Pierce framework as adapted for a participant starting from zero:

- **Skill assessment** — a participant-authored inventory of skills, interests, and what they want to build. Strengths-based; no deficit scoring, no grade, no file that follows them.
- **Gamified training** — opt-in levels with badges and visible progress, built so that stopping is never a loss and a level can always be re-entered. Progress is celebrated; it is never required.
- **Earn-to-learn stipends** — paid _while_ training, so learning is affordable in time as well as money. Paid on the same published calendar as everything else, with the same 48-hour confirmation.
- **Micro-enterprise seed support** — start-up support for participant-run work (creative services, culinary, growing, repair, care work, local trades), with the equipment provided rather than deducted and a simple gift-acknowledgment form reviewed by counsel.
- **Digital-economy access** — **equity is access, not just an invitation.** Hardware and connectivity are provided: high-spec laptop, smartphone, tablet, noise-canceling headphones, software and training budget, all as gifted equipment, never deducted from stipends, and **identical in the non-working model** because connectivity is a right, not a work tool.
- **Income targets (framework estimates — quadrupled):** lean ≈ $26,000+/mo against lean outgo ≈ $16,800/mo; premium ≈ $40,000+/mo against premium outgo ≈ $27,600/mo. Regenerative income is designed to **exceed** outgo so each participant builds their own rolling cushion — **lagniappe**: every need covered, plus a bit extra, plus help whenever it is needed.
- **Where the surplus goes:** the participant's own cushion **first**. Only then, voluntarily and downstream, does surplus feed the eight Regenerative Commons channels (education; healthcare facilities and prevention research; environmental conservation; community infrastructure; arts and culture; local economic development; community-led projects; regenerative R&D). Commons flow is vision, not commitment — and never assumed in anyone's budget math.
- **The income-floor layer:** the funding framework carries an income floor / regenerative-income seed layer of $100,000–$200,000 per participant inside the $1M+ envelope (framework estimate) — that is what bridges a participant's own income during the first year. It exists **so that the non-working model is genuinely free to be non-working.**

### 4.4 What the evidence supports, and what it does not

**Supported:** unconditional cash does not discourage work. Stockton SEED (125 residents, $500/month, 24 months, no work requirements, 2019–2021) saw full-time employment rise from 28% to 40%, month-to-month income volatility fall to 19% vs 26% for controls, and Kessler-10 distress move into the "likely well" range in year one. GiveDirectly's Kenya experiment (295 villages, ~$22.50/month arms and ~$500 lump sums) found businesses +14%, revenues +41%, net revenues +52%, **labor supply did not fall**, and mental health improved _more_ in the monthly-stream arms. A meta-analysis of 114 cash-transfer studies across 72 programs found strong positive effects on 10 of 13 outcomes and **no dependency effect**. Housing First keeping people housed is the strongest, most-replicated result in the field: 88% retention over five years at Pathways NY vs 47% traditional; 73% of days stably housed across five Canadian cities vs 32%; 2.46× more likely stably housed at 18–24 months in a four-RCT review. Finland's national policy cut long-term homelessness 35% from 2008 to 2015 and 68% from 2008 to 2022, saving society at least €15,000 per housed person per year (2011 estimate).

**Not supported, stated honestly:** the final peer-reviewed Stockton analysis did **not** find the employment gain statistically significant; benefits faded during COVID and after payments ended; housing alone does **not** reliably improve mental health, substance use, or employment — our housing + income combination is a design hypothesis whose components are each backed, not a proven package. Mexico-scale vs million-scale: **no study tests grants at our scale.** None of it proves anything about San Diego specifically.

## 5. ZERO-BARRIER UI ONBOARDING

The owner's framework, honored in spirit and in mechanics.

**1. Automatic enrollment with opt-out.**

- The **team identifies and enrolls eligible people.** Paperwork is done **by the team, not by the participant** — no forms to figure out alone, no document scavenger hunt, no online portal as the price of entry.
- Enrollment is coordinated through existing referral partners (shelter and housing partners, coordinated-entry pathways where applicable) — people already known to the system are found, not made to re-present themselves.
- **Opt-out is absolute and immediate:** any participant may leave at any time, for any reason or no reason, with no explanation required, no exit interview as a condition, and no effect on anything they have already received. Re-entry is always open.
- Identity and phone verification exist **to protect the participant from a mis-payment, never to gate their eligibility.** The team handles documents; the participant is not the filing clerk of their own survival.

**2. Direct and regular disbursement.**

- **Monthly is the default; weekly is available** if a participant prefers it. The cadence belongs to the participant.
- **One primary disbursement rail** for all routine payments, with exceptions documented and minimized (error control #7). Rail changes happen only at phase boundaries.
- **A published payment calendar** with fixed dates shared with participants and the sponsor in advance; any change announced at least 7 days ahead. Calendar adherence is reported as a metric.
- **A test payment confirmed received before any first full payment** (error control #2) — wrong account numbers are discovered at $1, not at full value.
- **Every disbursement is confirmed by the participant within 48 hours** through their chosen channel — call, text, or in person (error control #3). Confirmation is a service to the participant, never an obligation that can trigger a penalty.
- **One published grievance channel** — "my payment is wrong or missing" — acknowledged within 24 hours, resolved or escalated within 5 business days, with fix authority pre-delegated so a case-owner can re-issue without a committee (error control #6).

**3. Optional, non-judgmental financial literacy and peer mentoring.**

- Financial-literacy support and peer mentoring are **offered, never assigned**, and never a condition of anything.
- **Nothing about how a participant spends their money is collected, scored, or asked about.** No budgeting requirement, no receipts to submit for personal spending, no "money-management readiness" gate on anyone's stipend or their standing in the program.
- Peer mentors are paid participants, chosen by the cohort, with a structural firewall: they can support, never decide (§3).

## 6. SACRED PROTECTIONS

These are unconditional, non-negotiable program rules. They are not policies that flex with funding pressure, press interest, evaluation needs, or convenience. **Every staff member and every partner signs this code before the first day and again annually. A partner who violates it loses the partnership.** Anyone may raise a breach through the single grievance channel, and it is escalated directly to the sponsor's compliance owner, bypassing the person named.

### 6.1 SACRED PRIVACY

- **Zero surveillance of persons, families, or spiritual and religious life.** No monitoring of where a participant goes, who visits, what they believe, whether they attend services or meetings, or how they spend their time.
- **Data minimization:** nothing is stored beyond what the individual opts into. Payment and enrollment records exist because money must move; case notes are administrative, not personal; **no case file is a biography.** Records are reviewed quarterly and deleted by default.
- **Participant stories never become marketing content.** No personal narrative, image, or detail appears anywhere public without a separate, specific, revocable written consent for that specific use — and refusing costs nothing, ever.
- **No location tracking, no guest logging, no "wellness checks" as compliance.** A welfare concern is a human conversation, never a surveillance function.

### 6.2 NO PROFIT FROM INJURY OR PASSING

- **No monetization of any person's injury, death, or story — by staff, partners, or media, ever.** No fundraising appeal built on someone's worst day; no "before and after" imagery sold or licensed; no paid speaking, consulting, conference, or content revenue generated from a participant's hardship.
- **Celebration of contributions never becomes exploitation.** Celebrating what a participant built is consented, participant-authored, and consent-revocable. It is never a fundraising asset and never a debt of gratitude.
- **When a participant is hurt or dies, the program's public duty is dignity and silence, not narrative.** No statement, image, or case study is published from it. If the family asks for something said publicly, the family writes it.

### 6.3 NO LEVERAGING FAMILY

- **No member's support is ever contingent on another member's behavior.** No participant's housing, stipend, cleaning, connectivity, or pet care is tied to what a partner, child, parent, sibling, or friend does or does not do.
- **Reunification is by choice only.** There is no family-reunification requirement, no "earn your family back" pathway, and no reporting on family members' conduct. Family participation is invited, never required, and never the price of a home.
- **No member is used as leverage in any direction** — not to encourage behavior, not to secure compliance, not to demonstrate outcomes.

### 6.4 NO CAREER-BUILDING OFF HARM

- **No one builds a career, a credential, a portfolio, a thesis, a book, a consultancy, or a reputation off a participant's harm.** This binds staff, contractors, partners, evaluators, and volunteers identically.
- **Research and evaluation require participant consent, participant compensation, and separate governance approval** — and the participant sees anything written about them before it is filed.
- **Participant-facing roles are paid roles.** No unpaid "experience," no internships that trade dignity for a line on a résumé.

### 6.5 How these protections are held

- They are **not votable down** — not by the cohort council, not by a funder's preference, not by a program lead. Only a participant-governance vote plus owner ratification may ever amend them.
- **Every disbursement pause, wave gate, and audit** operates without ever touching a participant's housing or rights.
- Breach consequences are structural, not personal: partnership termination, removal from participant-facing work, and a published (non-identifying) correction in the monthly error/fix report.

## 1. HOUSEHOLD DEFINITION & COUNTING

### 1.1 What a household is

A **household** is the group of people who live as family together in one home — regardless of paperwork. It is defined by **who lives as family**, not by blood, marriage, guardianship, or a lease:

- a participant, their partner or spouse, or a chosen partner;
- their children, including adult children, stepchildren, and children in their care;
- **chosen family** — the people who function as family whether or not any legal relationship exists;
- kin in a caregiving relationship in either direction (an elder parent, an aunt, a grandchild being raised by a grandparent, a sibling with a disability);
- animals, who are family (DOC 1 §4.1, owner directive).

**A chosen-family household counts exactly the same as a legal or blood household.** No one is asked to prove a relationship. The program does not request marriage licenses, guardianship orders, or custody documents to establish household membership, and no one's membership depends on a document existing. The practical test is the household's own statement of who lives with them and lives as family.

**Animals are members of the household for accommodations and budget, not for enrollment, voting, or data.** They have an animal record (species, care needs, vet status, named caregiver-of-record), not a person file.

### 1.2 How a household counts against the 5–25

**Recommendation: a household occupies ONE participant slot in the cohort's 5–25, regardless of its size.** Each individual in the household is enrolled **individually** inside the household record — for their own consent, their own data, their own disbursement decisions, and their own council vote (§8) — but the household counts as one slot.

**Why (honest justification):**

1. **The 5–25 is a knowability number, not a headcount.** DOC 1 §1.1 says a cohort is "small enough that every participant is a known person to a named team," and §3 sets the caseload at ≤15 per case-owner. The number exists to protect the _relationship_ — one named human per file. Households, not bodies, are what a navigator holds.
2. **Headcount counting would punish families for existing.** If a family of five consumed five of twenty-five slots, the team would be under constant structural pressure to cap children, split households, or prefer single adults — in direct conflict with DOC 1's "a gate miss never de-funds anyone" (§2.3), the universal model ("everyone — a universal model, not a niche program"), and the no-leverage rules (§6.3). A counting rule that gives a mother a smaller share of the program because she has children is not a neutral accounting choice.
3. **Every individual is still enrolled and still counted.** Nothing is hidden: the slot is the _relationship unit_; the person is the _funding, consent, and rights unit_.

**The alternative, stated honestly.** Counting **each person as one slot** has two real merits: (a) the public plan's "60–300 participants" would unambiguously mean 60–300 _people_ rather than 60–300 households; (b) funding scales by person, so a slot count understates cost to any reader who assumes person. Under the recommendation, a cohort of 25 family households could cover 60–100+ people while still reading publicly as "25 participants." **The mitigation is mandatory, not optional:**

- **Always publish both numbers.** Every gate report, cost table, and public figure states **"X households / Y people."** Never one without the other. Same for the program-wide line: "12 seeds × 5–25 households = 60–300 households = _a range of people published each time it is quoted._"
- **A hard cap of 25 households per cohort** (the profile-carrying number), and a recommended practical band of **8–20 households** while the model is new, because family households are more work per slot (§1.3), not less.
- **The minimum of 5 may be met by households of any size**, and no cohort opens with fewer than 5 households or fewer than the partner coverage its pocket needs.

**No household is ever split across cohorts or pockets.** One household, one home, one housing arrangement, one pocket (DOC 1 §1.1).

### 1.3 Caseload weighting — how a household-heavy cohort keeps the ≤15 cap honest

Counting a household as one slot does **not** mean it is one unit of work. DOC 1's ≤15 caseload cap is a protection for participants; it must be measured in something that reflects the actual load.

**Recommendation: count caseload in weighted households.** Framework estimates, to be tested against real navigator time in Phase 1:

|Household type|Caseload weight (framework estimate, not verified)|
|---|---|
|Single adult|1.00|
|Two or more adults, no children|1.25|
|Household with one child|1.50|
|Household with two or more children|1.75|
|Household with an unpaid-care need being formalized as paid care work (§5.2)|+0.25|
|Active DV safety plan (§9)|**Coverage-first: assigned its own named navigator time, no weight cap**|

**Hard rules that follow:**

- **The ≤15 weighted caseload cap is never raised to absorb a bigger household.** If the weight exceeds what a navigator can hold, the cohort adds a navigator or shrinks the household count — never the reverse.
- A family-heavy cohort will need 2.0 navigator FTE at roughly **8–10 households**, not at 16 (framework estimate; DOC 1 §3 assumed 16). This is a real staffing-cost consequence of families, and it is named here rather than discovered later.
- Coverage buddies and leave cover are even more important with families: one absent navigator must never mean a child's allowance or a pet's care is late.

### 1.4 When a household changes shape

Households are not static. The rules:

- **New adult member joins.** They are enrolled individually (own consent call, own verification, own rail where they prefer, own test payment, own council vote, own cushion). **No existing member's base lines are reduced or re-qualified to make room.** The household slot does not change.
- **New child joins** (birth, placement, reunification the household chose). The child allowance, child connectivity right, and study-space spec begin on the next published payment date. No re-qualification.
- **A member leaves by choice.** Their individual lines end at their own request; the household's base lines are recalculated on the next calendar, never clawed back. No exit interview is required of the departing member, ever.
- **A household splits** (separation, divorce, a safety transfer). A split creates a **second household slot** if both want to remain. **Rule: no administrative cap ever displaces a person.** If that takes a cohort over its household cap, the cohort publishes the variance in the next gate report and adds caseload capacity — it does not choose which half to disenroll.
- **A member dies.** See §12 (no profit from injury or passing) and the dignity protocol: the household keeps the home, keeps every base line, and the program's public duty is silence, not narrative. How the future grant envelope of a deceased member is handled is a **counsel question**.

### 1.5 Youth-headed households (flagged, not solved)

Some households are headed by a minor — a 16- or 17-year-old parenting or living independently, or a sibling pair. **The program does not precondition housing on guardianship or on a legal status it cannot verify from a desk.** A minor who is their own household head is enrolled, housed, and given the same base package; legal capacity, consent, and whether an adult must co-sign anything are **counsel questions** (§15). This section exists so the case is not discovered on move-in day.

## 2. UNIT-SIZE MAPPING

### 2.1 Occupancy standard (our rule, more generous than market default)

1. **No adult shares a bedroom with someone they did not choose.** Ever.
2. **No child shares a bedroom with an unrelated adult.** Ever.
3. **Baseline: no more than two people per bedroom**, and children may share only by the household's own choice — never forced to fit a unit.
4. **Every school-age child gets a homework-private space** — a desk with task lighting and a door or acoustic separation where the unit allows (§3.4).
5. **Every household gets a decompression space** (DOC 1 autism build spec) and, where any adult works from home, a work zone distinguishable from the children's study space (remote-first is an owner directive; a home is a valid work base).
6. **A household is never offered a smaller unit than its standard because a bigger one was unavailable.** If the pocket has no suitable unit, the cohort waits, or the whole household chooses a different pocket together.

### 2.2 Unit-size ladder

|Household composition|Recommended unit|Acceptable minimum (household's informed choice)|
|---|---|---|
|1 adult|Studio|Studio|
|2 adults|1BR|Studio only if the household chooses and a work zone fits|
|1 adult + 1 child|2BR|1BR only if the household chooses **and** a study nook is built into the spec|
|2 adults + 1 child|2BR|2BR (not 1BR — three people, two sleeping spaces minimum)|
|2 adults + 2 children|3BR|2BR only if children share by the household's choice|
|3 adults|2BR|2BR|
|3 adults + 1–2 children|3BR|3BR|
|4+ adults, or 5+ people|4BR+|3BR only by the household's own choice|
|Any household with a live DV safety plan (§9)|Chosen for safety first; size requirement is secondary to a confidential address||

## 3. CHILDREN

### 3.1 Schooling enrollment support

Children in a Home Key household stay in school. The **navigator with the family-liaison skill** (§14) supports, at the household's request and never as a condition:

- **Enrollment and transfer:** getting each child enrolled or transferred, including mid-year transfers, without the family re-presenting itself as homeless at the front office unless the household chooses to (McKinney-Vento rights and how they interact with our program are a **school-law question for counsel** — we do not advise on it ourselves).
- **Records and immunizations:** retrieving records with the family's written authorization; coordinating with the health partner for well-child visits and immunization catch-up, **without ever becoming the source of a school's information about a child** beyond what the family authorizes.
- **Transportation:** zero-entropy travel applies to school. Youth free fare via SANDAG's Youth Opportunity Pass (free through June 2027, confirmed at sponsor level 2026-09-07) and PRONTO LIFE (a live program from October 1, 2026, search-verified 2026-09-07) enrollment support are delivered on site.
- **IEP/504 and learning support:** the navigator makes the referral to an education-rights advocate (**partner-side, not us** — §14) and does not evaluate, diagnose, or sit in place of a parent's advocate.
- **District boundaries:** each pocket's unit address determines district and school assignment, so **school-district boundary checks are done per pocket during the site walk**, before units are signed (open question §15).

### 3.2 The childcare co-op — parent-run, staffed by stipended participant roles

Childcare is the single largest hidden cost a family faces, and it is also a legitimate job. The model:

- **Parent-run.** The co-op belongs to the families using it. Schedules, rules, and hours are set by the participating parents, not by staff.
- **Staffed by stipended participant roles.** A **Childcare Co-op Coordinator** is a paid participant role (like peer mentors, DOC 1 §3) — paid at **stipend/wage terms, on the same published calendar, with the same 48-hour confirmation**. Additional caregiving hours are paid hours. **Care work is work and is never extracted unpaid.**
- **Structural firewall (carried from DOC 1).** The coordinator and co-op caregivers **never decide anyone's eligibility, payments, or program standing**, and hold no authority over any child's household.
- **No family is required to use it.** Families may use the co-op, a licensed partner, a relative, or nothing. **Co-op participation is never a condition of housing, stipend, or standing**, and non-use is never recorded as a deficiency.
- **Safety and compliance:** background checks, first aid/CPR, and safe-space rules are required wherever insurance or law requires them; the co-op's licensing threshold and liability posture (family-child-care rules, ratio requirements) are a **counsel-and-insurance question** before it opens (§15).
- **Accessibility:** sensory-friendly quiet space, allergy-aware snack rules, and pet-safe separation are build and operating requirements, because autism accommodations and animals do not stop at the household door.

### 3.3 Pediatric, well-child, and benefits enrollment via the Navigator's health partners

- **Pediatric and well-child care:** via the health partner (FQHC) referral path. Our Navigator lists **Family Health Centers of San Diego** (sliding fee by income and family size; no one turned away for inability to pay; dental and mental health included — checked 2026-09-04) and **San Ysidro Health** for city pockets (verified as an FQHC with city clinic sites, 2026-09-07). **Vista Community Clinic serves North County only** — relevant only if a pocket sits north (verified 2026-09-07). **We do not run a clinic** (DOC 1 §3).
- **Benefits enrollment:** Medi-Cal (including children's coverage), CalFresh (in the Navigator, household-size based, rolling enrollment, checked 2026-09-04), WIC, and school meal programs — enrollment supported by the navigator, decided by the family.
- **Honest gap:** **the Navigator today has no dedicated pediatric, WIC, school, or childcare entries.** Its 29 entries are adult-facing plus wellness-animal. A child-and-family resource sweep (pediatric clinics, WIC sites, school liaison contacts, libraries, playgrounds, after-school programs) is an explicit follow-up task — it is a _researcher_ deliverable, not something DOC 2 can claim exists.

### 3.4 Homework-private space and play space as build requirements

Added to the master framework's accommodation standards (alongside the autism and wellness-animal specs — **requirements, not upgrades**):

- **A study space per school-age child:** desk or table at the right height, task lighting, closed storage, and a quiet-closing door or acoustic separation where the unit allows. Priced inside CapEx at **$3,000–$8,000 per school-age child** (framework estimate — quadrupled).
- **Devices:** age-appropriate device and connection per child inside the child connectivity RIGHT ($200–$400/mo, framework estimate — quadrupled). Children's devices are gifted equipment, never deducted, and never conditional on school performance.
- **Play space:** indoor age-appropriate, sensory-aware play zone, safe from pets by design (gates, storage for pet gear) and safe for pets (no small-parts hazard); outdoor space child-safe where the unit has it. Pocket-level play resources (parks, libraries, rec centers) are mapped during the site walk — a Navigator gap today.
- **Sensory and routine supports for children** use the same standard as the autism spec: predictable lighting, labeled storage, visual routine cards, transition supports at arrival and departure. A child's sensory needs do not require a diagnosis to be honored.

### 3.5 MANDATORY REPORTING vs SACRED PRIVACY — the boundary, written plainly

This is the hardest question in DOC 2 and it gets a direct answer, not a slogan.

**What is true, stated first:**

- **Privacy is the default and is maximal within the law.** DOC 1 §6.1 applies with full force to children and elders: no surveillance, no guest logging, no "wellness checks" as compliance, no case file as biography, data reviewed quarterly and deleted by default.
- **Mandatory-reporting duties are law. They are not waiverable.** Where the law places a duty to report suspected child abuse or neglect, or elder/dependent-adult abuse, on a person in a role like ours, the program cannot waive it, a participant cannot waive it, a funder cannot waive it, and a cohort council vote cannot waive it. **We do not pretend otherwise, and we do not write a privacy promise we cannot keep.**
- **The program never adds surveillance beyond what the law requires.** No home inspections beyond what the lease or law requires. No compliance home visits. No monitoring of who is in a home. No gathering of information for reporting's sake. Anything we are told is treated as told in confidence until the law's threshold is met — and the threshold is not "this family worries me."
- **Participants are told in plain language at onboarding, before enrollment.** The notice names, in the participant's own language: that staff carry reporting duties; what kinds of situations the law covers; that a report would be made by a named person, not anonymously by "the program"; what the family can expect afterward; and that they may bring anyone they choose to the conversation. Written notice plus a spoken walkthrough, with the offer of a support person present. **Nobody signs into this without knowing it.**
- **If a report is made:** the family is told that a report was made and by whom, unless the law forbids it; the family is offered support (a DV advocate, a legal-aid referral, a warm handoff to the child-welfare agency's own family-support path where one exists); and **the report never becomes leverage.** A report is never a reason to reduce, pause, or condition housing, stipend, cleaning, connectivity, pet care, or council membership for any member.
- **No retaliation, no surveillance creep.** After a report, nothing about the household's data collection, visit cadence, or payment treatment changes.

**The counsel question this raises (flagged, not answered here):** exactly which of our staff are mandated reporters by role; under which code sections; the difference between what a staff member personally observes and what a participant discloses to them; duties relating to elders and dependent adults; and whether our program's own recordkeeping creates any additional reporting duty we have not anticipated. **Counsel answers this before the first family is enrolled**, and the plain-language onboarding notice is drafted _by counsel_ and reviewed by the cohort council for comprehensibility. **General legal landscape only — this document does not assert specific statutory scope, which is why it is a counsel question and not a copy question.**

## 4. REUNIFICATION BY CHOICE ONLY

### 4.1 Offered, never required

- **Reunification is the household's decision, full stop.** The program may provide support — travel, transport, a mediator the household chooses, help with a phone number, a family visit space in a shared room — **when the household asks.**
- **There is no reunification requirement, no reunification pathway with an entrance criterion, no "earn your family back" track, and no reunification metric in any gate report.** A cohort is never measured on how many families it puts back together.
- **The program never contacts a family member on a participant's behalf** without the participant's specific, revocable written request — and never contacts anyone to ask about a participant's conduct.
- **Housing never depends on an outcome.** No court order, no agency determination, no relative's decision, and no mediation result changes anyone's housing, stipend, rights, or standing.

### 4.2 The no-leverage audit (how DOC 1 §6.3 is checked, not just stated)

A rule that is never checked is a hope. The audit:

- **Quarterly, conducted by the sponsor's compliance owner with the cohort council reviewing the method and the results**, and published in the monthly error/fix report **including quarters with zero findings** (silence is not a metric — DOC 1 §7).
- **Method:** (1) file sampling — pull household files and read every payment/standing record for any reference to another member's behavior, a court outcome, or an agency decision; (2) participant confirmation — a standing question in monthly feedback, asked in a way that does not require a participant to name their own family; (3) staff interviews — every navigator is asked the same question, in writing, each quarter; (4) the grievance channel as a fourth intake.
- **Any occurrence is a breach report**, not a coaching note: partnership termination, removal from participant-facing work, and a published non-identifying correction (DOC 1 §6.5).
- **The audit's four questions, verbatim:** _Does any member's housing, payment, service, or standing change because of another member's behavior? Is any member asked to influence another member? Is any member's file used as evidence about another member? Is any member's participation a condition of another member's support?_ Four "no"s, or the finding is published.

### 4.3 The family-court interface

- **The program is not a party, an informant, or an expert.** Staff do not attend hearings, do not call agencies about a member's conduct, and do not write declarations — unless compelled by law, in which case the compliance owner and counsel handle it and the participant is told first.
- **What the program does at a participant's written request:** provides a factual housing-verification letter (address withheld where safety requires — §9); coordinates with the participant's _own_ counsel; refers to legal aid; and keeps paying every line on the published calendar through whatever proceeding is underway.
- **A custody or dependency outcome never changes a household's base lines.** If a household's composition changes after a court decision, §1.4 applies: lines recalculate, nothing is clawed back, no one is displaced by an administrative rule.
- **Children's own records and wishes:** we do not report on a child's statements to any party. What a child tells a staff member is treated with the §3.5 boundary — privacy within the law, told to the family in plain language.

### 4.4 Counsel referral, not legal advice

**Staff never give legal advice** — not on custody, not on guardianship, not on immigration, not on benefits, not on district boundaries. The program **refers** to counsel and legal-aid partners, and the referral is tracked like any other pillar service. If no legal-aid partner is available for a DMV-level question, that is a **partner gap**, not a reason for a staff member to improvise advice.

## 5. MULTI-ADULT, ELDER, AND KINSHIP HOUSEHOLDS

### 5.1 Per-member enrollment and per-member accommodations

- **Every adult in a household is enrolled individually:** their own consent call, their own identifiers, their own rail where they prefer it (§10), their own confirmation obligations, their own council vote (§8), their own cushion (§7.2). **Nobody is enrolled "under" another adult**, and no adult's enrollment is contingent on another's.
- **Disability accommodation is mapped per member, not per household.** The intake records, at the member's choice, mobility needs, sensory needs, cognitive and communication needs, chronic illness, medical equipment requirements, and in-home care needs — and the unit's build spec is adjusted to the people in it: grab bars and roll-in/walk-in shower where needed, single-level sleeping where possible, non-slip surfaces, predictable lighting, space and power for home medical equipment (oxygen, dialysis, mobility-device charging/storage), temperature control, and storage for medical supplies. Priced at **$4,000–$12,000 per member who needs it**, inside CapEx (framework estimate — quadrupled).
- **Elders and dependent adults are not "cases."** Their care is the household's decision; their rights are their own; their records are their own; and the §3.5 mandatory-reporting boundary and the §6.1 privacy protections apply to them explicitly and equally.
- **Multi-generational households get the unit standard they need**, not the unit the market has left over.

### 5.2 Elder care and kinship caregiving: care work IS contribution, and it is paid

A grandmother raising three grandchildren, an adult child managing a parent's care, a sibling supporting a sibling — in the housing system as it exists, that work is invisible and unpaid, and the caregiver is often penalized for it. In this model it is recognized and paid.

- **A paid kinship-care role.** Where a household member provides ongoing care for another member (a child, an elder, a disabled adult), that member may be enrolled in a **paid caregiving role** — recognition, not employment. Paid at **$2,000–$4,000/mo** (framework estimate — quadrupled), on the same published calendar and 48-hour confirmation as everything else. **Care work counts as a contribution to the household and the cohort, exactly as a job would.**
- **No means test and no proof of hours.** We do not audit caregiving. Hours are self-declared, because **a surveillance regime on a caregiver would violate DOC 1 §6.1 and turn recognition into extraction with extra steps.** What is verified is the payment, like every other payment.
- **The role carries no authority.** Like peer mentors and the childcare coordinator, a paid caregiver never decides eligibility, payments, or standing — for their own household or anyone else's. They cannot be made a gatekeeper over the person they care for.
- **Paid care never replaces a right.** It is an addition to the household's base lines, never a substitute for cleaning, connectivity, or a member's own stipend.
- **Respite is protected.** A caregiver may use program-supported respite (co-op hours, a hired worker, a partner service) without any explanation and without losing the care-care line.
- **When care work is not what the person wants, it stops** — the household decides, no notice period, no penalty, and the person receiving care keeps every base line.

### 5.3 Stipend coverage per member (summary rule)

**Every adult gets the per-adult base stipend. Every child gets the child allowance. Every animal gets pet care. Caregivers get the care-work line. No member is a "dependent" who receives fewer rights than anyone else.** Sizes are in §11.

## 6. ANIMALS AT HOUSEHOLD SCALE

### 6.1 Multiple animals

- **Bands scale per animal:** pet care **$600–$1,200/mo per animal**; pet setup **$2,000–$6,000 per animal** (DOC 1's band read as per-animal); the first animal's care sits inside the per-adult variable band, each additional animal adds a line.
- **Honest adjust:** food, litter, and insurance are somewhat cheaper per animal in multi-animal homes than the band assumes. **The bands flex — the same language DOC 1 uses for breed, size, and need.** At 90 days, actual spend inside each band is reviewed with the household and the band is revised down honestly if it is padded beyond need (padding is not a reason to overspend; it is a reason not to be caught short).
- **CapEx:** a three-animal household uses three setup lines; that is one reason a household with several animals prices near the top of its CapEx band. Multi-animal households are asked their animal count **at enrollment**, not discovered at move-in.
- **Build spec scales:** crate/den space per animal ("home, never punishment" — DOC 1 §4.1), secure patio/run for the dogs, scratch and climbing structures for cats, washable covers and durable flooring throughout, and a separate animal zone where a household needs a child-safe or sensory-safe separation.
- **Every animal has a named caregiver-of-record** inside the household, and a named backup — so an animal is never left uncared for because the household's primary caregiver is hospitalized, jailed, or overwhelmed.

### 6.2 Service animals vs emotional support animals under the May 2026 federal narrowing

**The honest flag, carried from the Navigator (checked 2026-09-05):** HUD's FHEO enforcement guidance of **May 22, 2026** pursues animal-accommodation complaints **only** for animals individually trained to perform disability-related tasks. Untrained emotional support animals are **no longer prioritized by HUD enforcement** — while Fair Housing Act and Section 504 obligations and private legal action still exist. The Navigator entry (`hud-assistance-animals`) carries this status honestly.

**Our posture, stated plainly:**

1. **Wellness animals are requirements of this model regardless of the federal enforcement posture** (owner directive). We acquire, lease, and renovate pet-friendly units we control, so **a landlord's FHA analysis never gates whether a participant's animal comes home.** We do not depend on the ESA/service distinction to house animals, which is exactly why the narrowing does not weaken our model.
2. **The distinction matters the moment a household moves into housing we do not control** (a private-market unit, a sublease, a family member's building) or when a landlord disputes an animal. Then it is: individually trained disability-related task = strongest protection; ESA = weaker protection and shrinking federal enforcement, though state and local protections and private action vary.
3. **The Navigator flags this honestly at the point of use** and the navigator explains the difference **before** a household relies on it — never after a denial.
4. **We do not fabricate ESA letters, and no staff member writes one.** A supporting letter comes from a treating clinician, on their own judgment. A program that manufactures accommodation letters to make paperwork work would be committing the kind of small fraud that costs the whole team's credibility (and the owner's) — and it is not what a wellness-animal policy means.
5. **Multiple animals and breed/size:** our units have no breed or size restriction. Where a household moves to housing we do not control, the navigator maps what that housing actually allows before the household commits.

### 6.3 Hospital stays, foster, and boarding — with reunion as the goal

- **Safety-net foster is a proven partner path.** San Diego Humane Society's Safety Net Foster Program provides free temporary foster while an owner deals with **housing insecurity, domestic violence, or a hospital stay**, provides all care supplies, takes up to 3 pets, and aims to reunite within 90 days (Navigator entry, checked 2026-09-05 — capacity limited, $30 microchip/license at reclaim, no visits during foster, extensions past 30 days not guaranteed). **Those limits are stated to households up front**, not discovered at intake.
- **Program-funded boarding is the overflow line.** Pet care has headroom; where safety-net foster is full or unsuitable, the program pays for boarding, a pet sitter, or a household-nominated caregiver, **funded from the household's pet care budget, not from the participant's cushion.**
- **Reunion is the goal, always.** No animal is surrendered, rehomed, or euthanized as a cost-saving measure, and **no animal is used as leverage** to influence a participant's decisions, treatment, or cooperation (§6.3 of DOC 1, extended here to animals — animals are members, and leverage through a family member's animal is leverage through the family member).
- **A standing plan exists before move-in:** caregiver-of-record, backup, the vet and microchip record, the household's preferences for what happens during a hospital stay, and who may not take the animal. The Vet/Pet Liaison holds it; the household can change it at any time.
- **The animal's own welfare outcomes are recorded** (vet status, pantry access, any foster episode and its reunion outcome) and reported in the cohort's gate report — DOC 1 already made animal-welfare outcomes an explicit gate metric; DOC 2 adds that **multi-animal and hospitalization episodes are reported per household**, including episodes with zero animals surrendered.

## 7. HOUSEHOLD STIPEND CONSTRUCTION

### 7.1 The construction rule

A household's support is built from named parts, never a flat pooled number:

**Household monthly support = fixed OpEx (rent residual + utilities + cleaning RIGHT + connectivity RIGHT) + per-adult variable stipends + per-child allowances + per-animal pet care + care-work stipend (where earned).**

- **Everything is flexible to circumstance**, per the owner's directive — the bands flex per member, per month, under a published floor. One adult's medical month does not eat another adult's band, because the bands are separate lines inside one file.
- **Cleaning and connectivity are RIGHTS at the household line** and are never reduced — not when a member leaves, not when a report is filed, not when a court case is pending, not when a funder asks for savings (DOC 1 §4.1).
- **A member's line is a member's line.** Adding a member never shrinks an existing member's line, and no adult's line is a household total divided by headcount.

### 7.2 Lagniappe ownership — whose cushion is it?

**Recommended rule, and the reason for it:**

- **Each adult's cushion is their own.** It cannot be spent, signed for, redirected, counted, or claimed by another adult — not a partner, not a parent, not a household head. A cushion is not a household asset and not a shared account.
- **Children's cushions are stewarded for the child.** The child's share is held and used **in the child's interest** — education, a first deposit, equipment, a business, health, a car — with an adult steward of record (guardian or a trusted steward the household names) and with the child told, at an age-appropriate level, what is theirs and what happened to it. **At majority, the remaining cushion is the child's own**, structured correctly (counsel question: minors cannot hold substantial grants outright; a trust, UTMA-type arrangement, or program-held education-and-first-home fund is the likely vehicle, and that choice is legal work, not copy).
- **Nobody's cushion is another adult's spending money.** A partner cannot draw on it, a parent cannot direct it, and **no staff member may direct any cushion** for any purpose. Staff explain the options; the owner of the cushion decides.
- **No household member's cushion may be used to cover a program cost.** If money is tight, that is a program problem solved inside the bands and the remediation line, never by raiding a member's cushion.
- **What is shared is what the program pays for**, not what members have saved. Shared costs (rent, utilities, cleaning, connectivity) are program lines. A household may of course choose to pool its own cushions — **but only by each adult's own uncoerced choice, and the pool must be dissolvable at any adult's sole request.**
- **In a DV safety-planning household, cushion ownership is the protective default (§9):** the at-risk adult's cushion is reachable only by that adult, full stop.

### 7.3 What staff may never do with a household's money

- Never require a budget, receipts, or proof of spending on personal items (DOC 1 §5.3).
- Never treat one adult as the "responsible" payee for another adult's line.
- Never make a disbursement conditional on another member's cooperation or behavior (§6.3).
- Never pool lines without each member's written, revocable consent, and never as a default.
- Never use money cadence or amount as behavioral pressure. Amounts change only when circumstances change — or when the participant chooses.

## 8. HOUSEHOLD GOVERNANCE & CONSENT

### 8.1 One adult, one vote

- **Each adult in a household holds their own cohort-council vote** on every matter in DOC 1 §7 (shared-space rules, activities calendar, staff hiring input, celebrations, feedback agenda, local commons use). A household's slot does not dilute its adults' votes, and **a household does not vote as a bloc.**
- **No adult speaks for another adult.** No member may be registered as another member's spokesperson, proxy, or representative. If a household wants to share a view, that is the household's business; the council hears members individually.
- **No member is silenced by another's presence.** If being in the same room as another member is unsafe or intolerable, the affected member votes by a channel the facilitator arranges — written ballot, online, or 1:1 with the community liaison — and **the reason is recorded nowhere.** Under a DV safety plan (§9), seating, timing, and even attendance at the same meeting are arranged so the at-risk member's participation is protected, including keeping the other member away from that meeting.
- **Minors do not hold binding votes** (they are not responsible for money or legal compliance), **and they are not voiceless** — see §8.2.

### 8.2 Minors' participation: assent plus guardian consent, and a youth circle

- **Two consents, not one.** A minor's participation in any activity, service, or program is gated by **(a) the guardian's consent where law requires it and (b) the minor's own assent.** **A minor's "no" is honored.** A guardian's yes never overrides a child who does not want to take part in something — activities, publicity, sharing, counseling beyond what law or urgent safety requires.
- **Assent is revocable at any time**, restated in an age-appropriate way each year, and never treated as a behavior problem when withdrawn.
- **A youth circle** for ages 12–17 (with younger children's views routed through their guardians and, where appropriate, a counselor): chaired with staff support, it puts issues to the cohort council, and **every item gets a published response within one cycle** — a response, not necessarily the outcome. Its scope is what young people actually live: shared space, activities, food, safety, transport, school support, animals. **It is advisory, its agenda is set by the youth, and it is not the compliance or financial decision-making body.**
- **Children are never asked to testify about their own household** to a council, a staff member, an evaluator, or a visitor. Evaluation of children requires separate governance approval, the guardian's consent, the child's assent, and the child sees anything written about them before it is filed (DOC 1 §6.4, extended to minors).

### 8.3 Data minimization inside a family

**The rule: a parent does not get a child's file as their own. A partner does not get a partner's file. An adult child does not get a parent's file.**

- **Every member's data is their own record**, with guardian access only where guardianship law requires it and only to the extent it requires.
- **Children's records** (allowance and payment, health, school, counseling, case notes) are minimized to what is needed to deliver a service, held under the same quarterly-review-and-delete-by-default rule as DOC 1 §6.1, and never used as a household dossier. **A child's case file is not a transcript of the family's parenting.**
- **A guardian's access to a teenager's health or counseling information** (where state law gives teens their own confidentiality) follows the law, not our preference — a **counsel question** we owe families an answer to before enrollment, told to them in plain language.
- **Payments:** the guardian/steward of record confirms a child's allowance disbursement (§10.3) but does not see or control another adult's line.
- **No photographs or identifying images of minors anywhere public — ever — regardless of any guardian's consent.** This is our own rule, deliberately stricter than law requires.
- **A family member's story is not another member's promotion.** No member — adult or child — appears in any public material because another member consented. Each member's consent is their own, specific, and revocable (DOC 1 §6.1).
- **Under a DV safety plan, the reversal in §9 controls:** information defaults to the at-risk member only, and the other adult's access is cut to the legal minimum.

### 8.4 Household decisions belong to the household

Move, don't move, which unit, who lives with them, how the household spends its own cushions, whether to work, who speaks at a meeting, what a child's activities are, whether the co-op is used, whether the front door gets a keypad: **household decisions are the household's.** Staff support, inform, and execute — they do not decide.

**Three narrow exceptions, named honestly:**

1. **Legal and lease compliance** (occupancy limits, safety codes) — law, not staff preference.
2. **Safety** (§9) — a safety plan may override a household decision when a member is at risk, and that override is documented and reviewed.
3. **Money movement** — the disbursement rail is chosen by the member(s) it pays to (§10), and staff execute it; staff never redirect it on their own judgment.

**Adding a household member never requires re-qualification and never reduces another member's lines** (§1.4). If a lease's occupancy limit is the binding constraint, that is a housing-search and counsel problem — never a reason to leave a family member outside.

## 9. DOMESTIC VIOLENCE & SAFETY PLANNING (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

**This section overrides every sequencing rule elsewhere in DOC 1 and DOC 2.** If safety and a normal process conflict, safety wins, and the process waits. The rule below is absolute: **no one's housing, money, access, or standing is ever conditioned on staying near, being reachable by, or accounting to anyone who poses a risk to them.**

### 9.1 Confidential placement and address protection

- **Confidential placement.** A household with a DV safety plan is placed in a unit whose address is **not shared with the cohort, the council, other participants, the neighborhood liaison's public materials, or any partner** — only with the people who need it to deliver the home.
- **Mail:** program correspondence goes to a partner-fronted address, a PO box, or an address the survivor chooses. **No program mail, no branded mail, no package with our name on it arrives at a protected address unless the household asks for it.**
- **Records:** the address is stored only where legally unavoidable (lease, utilities, benefits), redacted everywhere else, and **never written in a case note as a street address** — "unit," not "1428 Elm." No cohort directory exists at all, which is the strongest form of address protection.
- **Staff conduct — the hard rules:** no staff member confirms, denies, hints, or speculates about where any participant lives, to anyone, including another participant, a relative, a partner agency, a funder, an evaluator, or the press. **Every request for a location — from anyone, including someone claiming to be family — goes to the compliance owner, never to the case-owner**, and is refused unless compelled by law, in which case counsel and the survivor are in the room first.
- **Pocket exposure:** no pocket is announced publicly until units are secured (DOC 1 §2.2), and **a DV-safe unit's pocket is never announced at all** beyond the minimum publishing requirement.
- **The safety plan is the survivor's document, not the program's.** We do not store it, we do not grade it, and we do not require a police report, a restraining order, or any documentation at all as a condition of protection. **A survivor is believed on their own word.**

### 9.2 The no-leverage rule when someone asks for a location

- **The program discloses nothing** about a participant's whereabouts to any third party, including a person claiming to be a spouse, partner, parent, or guardian of the survivor, and including in response to informal requests from agencies, unless legally compelled — and then only after the survivor is told and counsel is engaged.
- **We do not relay messages from anyone to a protected household.** No letters, no phone numbers, no "he just wants to talk." If a survivor wants contact, the survivor arranges it.
- **We do not confirm or deny** that a person is or was a participant. Not to a landlord, not to an employer, not to a partner, not to a public records request without counsel.
- **A participant's absence from cohort activity is never investigated**, and the community liaison's normal reach-out behavior is suspended for a DV household unless the household initiates.

### 9.3 Fast-transfer pathway

- **Triggered by the household's word alone.** No documentation, no police report, no proof, no review committee, no wait for a council meeting.
- **Timeline (framework estimates, targets to be set with the DV partner):** interim safe placement within **72 hours** where the household wants to leave immediately; permanent safe unit within **14–30 days**.
- **Nothing is lost.** The household keeps the identical CapEx band, unit standard, fixed OpEx, cleaning and connectivity RIGHTS, per-member stipends, child allowances, care-work line, pet setup and pet care, and cohort council seats. **A safety transfer is not a downgrade and never costs a line.** Where an interim placement is a hotel or partner unit, the program pays, **pets included**, and no line is reduced while a permanent unit is found.
- **The old address is not "held" for them.** The household is not asked to keep a lease, a utility, or a mailbox at a location that is unsafe.
- **Enrollment and verification follow the move**, not the other way around. A person in danger can be moved before their file is complete — the file is completed later, and the missing paperwork is never a reason to delay.
- **The receiving pocket must accept the household even if it is over its household cap** — safety placements do not count against a cohort's cap until the next gate report, where the variance is published (never the identifying detail).

### 9.4 DV-advocate partnership — the sixth partner type

**Added to the partner brief list as Brief F (DV / survivor-safety partners), alongside shelter/housing, food, health, transit, and animal welfare.**

- **WHAT WE ASK OF THIS PARTNER:** (1) a confidential referral pathway with a named advocate contact and an agreed no-wrong-door process; (2) **safety-planning consultation for our staff** on individual cases, without the advocate taking on our caseload; (3) training for navigators and the community liaison in trauma-informed response, address-protection practice, and the "believe the survivor" standard; (4) guidance on interim safe placement capacity in the city.
- **WHAT THE PARTNER GAINS:** Navigator co-listing with source credit and check dates; participants arriving with padded support (housing, connectivity, stipends, pet care — including animals, a known barrier to leaving) rather than arriving with nothing; joint evidence on keeping survivors and their animals housed together; and a partner whose staff are trained before they call.
- **WHAT THIS IS NOT:** not a replacement for the partner's funding; not a funded program yet (Phase 0); no placement, timeline, or dollar commitments; **and the program does not become a DV service provider.** We refer, we protect, we move people, and we never improvise advocacy we are not trained for.
- **Structural requirement:** the advocate's involvement is **the survivor's choice**, and the advocate never becomes a condition of housing or a source of information about the survivor to us.

### 9.5 Safety planning overrides normal enrollment sequences — stated as a rule

**Rule: in any conflict between §9 and any other sequencing rule in DOC 1 or DOC 2, §9 prevails.** Safety placement before enrollment; safety placement before verification; safety placement before the council; safety placement before the unit meets full accommodation standards (safety first, then bring the unit up to standard in place, without moving the household again unless they choose to). **New disbursements do not pause for a safety transfer even if a non-negotiable error control is mid-repair** — instead, the test payment and confirmation controls run on the new rail in parallel, with the compliance owner personally tracking it.

**The one question this section cannot answer alone:** if both adults hold tenancy rights in the same unit, protecting one may require a legal process against the other (a protective order, an eviction, a master-lease structure that allows a rapid re-assignment). **That is a counsel question with a deadline — it must be answered before the first DV transfer happens, not during it** (§15).

## 10. HOUSEHOLD ERROR CONTROLS

DOC 1's eight error controls (from the entity/efficiency specs) all apply. Households make four of them harder and one of them easier.

### 10.1 One payment file per household, still single-owner

- **One household payment file, one named case-owner.** No orphan files, no shared ownership, no "either navigator handled it." The named case-owner signs every disbursement request for the household.
- **Inside the single file: one sub-ledger per adult, one per child, one per animal, plus the household lines.** The file is single-owner; the _lines_ are individual. This is the whole point — a household file that pools everyone's money into one undifferentiated number is how one member's money disappears.
- **Coverage buddy named on the file** for leave and after-hours (DOC 1 §3), and a household with a DV plan has a **second named backup cleared for address access.**

### 10.2 Rails — where the household wants separate rails

- **Default: one primary household rail** for fixed lines (error control #7 — single rail minimizes rail-switch error).
- **Documented exception: per-adult rails.** Where a household prefers separate rails — and especially where a safety plan requires it — each adult has their own rail. Exception documented with written reason and dual approval, reviewed at phase boundaries, and **never a count against anyone's standing.**
- **Hard rule: no adult's rail is controlled by another adult.** No member's money lands in another member's account without that member's own specific, revocable consent. In a DV household, this is not a preference — it is the protection.
- **The child allowance** goes to the named steward of record on the household's chosen rail, with the child's share tracked separately (§7.2).

### 10.3 Who confirms what

- **Each adult confirms their own disbursements** within 48 hours through their chosen channel (call, text, in person) — exactly as in DOC 1, per person, not per household.
- **The steward of record confirms the child allowance** and the child-connectivity line. **The confirmation record shows the child's share, not a pooled number**, and the child is told about it age-appropriately.
- **The named caregiver-of-record confirms pet-related lines** for their animal.
- **A household's confirmation is not valid if only one adult confirms for everyone.** A file where one adult repeatedly confirms another adult's money is flagged to the compliance owner as a possible control failure — and investigated as a possible coercion situation, not just an accounting error. **An adult who cannot or will not confirm their own line keeps that line pending without penalty** — it is never downgraded, never reassigned to another adult, and never a reason to pause the household's other lines.

### 10.4 Test payments and new payees

- **Every new payee and every new rail gets a micro-deposit test before any full disbursement** (error control #2). **Every adult added to a household is a new payee** — one test each. A household rail change is a new rail — one test. **No exceptions for "trusted" households and no exceptions for urgency, except a DV safety transfer**, where the inter-agency process for interim funds (§9.5) runs on a pre-tested emergency rail and the compliance owner personally tracks it.
- **Test-payment failures route to the fix path (#6), never to a blind retry** — a failed test for one household member never delays the rest of the household's lines.

### 10.5 Household disputes are not payment disputes

If two adults disagree about money, the program does not adjudicate, does not pick a side, and does not freeze lines as pressure. **The rule:** every line already owed is paid to its own owner on the calendar; the disputed item is handled through a written household decision or, in a safety situation, through §9. **No adult can use the program to reach another adult's money, and no staff member can be talked into it.**

### 10.6 What still holds from DOC 1

Published calendar with 7 days' notice on changes; grievance channel acknowledged within 24 hours and resolved or escalated within 5 business days, with fix authority pre-delegated; reconciliation weekly in Phase 1; monthly error/fix report **including zero-error months**; **pausing new disbursements when a non-negotiable control lapses never touches housing, cleaning, connectivity, pet care, or an existing stipend — and never touches them for one member because of another member's file.**

## 12. SACRED PROTECTIONS AT HOUSEHOLD SCALE

DOC 1 §6's four prohibitions stand unchanged — they are not votable down, they bind every staff member, partner, contractor, evaluator, and volunteer, and they are signed before the first day and annually. **DOC 2 extends each one to the household, and the extension is where a family raises the stakes.**

### 12.1 Sacred privacy — extended to children and elders explicitly

- Children and elders have the same absolute protection as every adult: **zero surveillance; data minimization (their records are not their parent's or their caregiver's); no story as marketing content; no location tracking, no guest logging, no "wellness checks" as compliance.**
- **A minor's image never appears in public material — ever — regardless of guardian consent.** Our rule, stricter than law.
- **One member's consent never exposes another member.** A household member's story, image, or detail cannot be published on anyone else's consent.
- **A guardian's access to a teenager's records is limited by law and by the teen's own assent** — a parent does not get a child's file as their own.
- **The DV exception (§12.5) reverses the default:** in a DV household, information flows **to the at-risk member only.**

### 12.2 No profit from any member's injury or passing — extended to every member

- **No monetization of any member's injury, death, or story** — including a child's, including an elder's, including an animal's. No fundraising appeal built on a family's worst day; no "before and after" imagery; no paid speaking, consulting, conference, or content revenue from a member's hardship.
- **When any member is hurt or dies:** the program's public duty is **dignity and silence**, not narrative. If the family asks for something said publicly, **the family writes it.** How a deceased member's Future Fund envelope is handled is a counsel question (§15) — and no line is clawed back from the survivors.
- **Celebrating contributions stays consented, member-authored, and revocable** — and never becomes a debt of gratitude. A child's drawing is not program content.

### 12.3 No leveraging any member — extended so leverage through a member is also banned

- **No member's support is ever contingent on another member's behavior** — not a partner's, not a child's, not a parent's, not an animal's.
- **Leverage through a member is banned too:** using a person's child, partner, elder, or animal to influence that person's decisions, treatment, cooperation, or testimony **is the same violation** as conditioning their housing. So is using one member's cushion, one member's line, or one member's file as pressure on another.
- **No member is used as leverage in any direction** — not to encourage behavior, not to secure compliance, not to demonstrate outcomes. **No member is a compliance tool.**
- **The quarterly no-leverage audit (§4.2) is the check**, published including zero-finding quarters.

### 12.4 No career-building off harm — extended

- Binds staff, contractors, partners, evaluators, and volunteers identically. **No thesis, book, consultancy, credential, or reputation built on a household's harm.**
- **Research involving children requires separate governance approval, the guardian's consent, the child's assent, participant compensation, and the child sees anything written about them before it is filed.** Children are never "cases" in a study.
- **Participant-facing roles are paid roles** — at every scale, including the childcare co-op, care work, and peer mentor roles in this document.

### 12.5 DV safety as the exception that reverses information-sharing defaults

- **Normally:** information about a household member flows within the household subject to §8.3's data minimization.
- **Under a live DV safety plan: the default reverses.** Information about the at-risk member goes **to the at-risk member only.** The other member's access is cut to the legal minimum. Address, mail, records, staff conduct, and even meeting seating serve the at-risk member first.
- **The program never discloses a location**, never relays a message, never confirms participation, and never investigates an absence (§9.2) — the no-leverage rule with no exceptions.
- **Safety overrides every sequence** (§9.5). Safety is the one thing that is not a vote, not a metric, and not a cost line.

### 12.6 How these are held (unchanged from DOC 1 §6.5)

Not votable down. Breaches escalate through the single grievance channel, past the person named, to the sponsor's compliance owner. Consequences are structural: partnership termination, removal from participant-facing work, and a published non-identifying correction in the monthly error/fix report. **Every disbursement pause, wave gate, and audit operates without ever touching a member's housing or rights.**

## 15. HONEST OPEN QUESTIONS

What this document does **not** answer. The first five are the ones the task asked to name plainly; the rest are what surfaced while writing it.

**The five named questions:**

2. **School-district boundary checks per pocket.** District and school assignment follow the unit address, so each pocket's school options must be verified **during the site walk, before units are signed**. Our Navigator has no school or district data today.
3. **Child-welfare interaction.** The exact scope of our staff's mandatory-reporting duties, which staff are mandated reporters by role, the elder/dependent-adult duties, the observe-vs-disclose distinction, and the plain-language onboarding notice — **all counsel-drafted before the first family enrolls** (§3.5). 3. **Tenancy rights in a safety transfer** — how to protect one adult when another holds tenancy in the same unit (protective orders, lease structure, master-lease re-assignment). **Counsel, with a deadline: this must be answered before the first DV transfer, not during it** (§9.5).

## 17. THE TWO MODELS, FOR A FAMILY

**The two models, for a family.** A family gets one home, sized to the family, with every base need covered the same way whether anyone in it works or not: no rent and no eviction clock; cleaning and connectivity held as permanent rights; a flexible stipend for every adult and an allowance for every child; pet setup and pet care for every animal, because animals are family; accommodations built for autism, disability, and elders; and a private place for every child to do homework. Every adult is their own participant — their own consent, their own rail if they choose it, their own vote on the cohort council, and their own cushion that no other adult and no staff member can touch; every child's share is stewarded for the child. The only difference between the two models is that the working model has regenerative-income pathways active and the non-working model does not — and a family can move between them at any time, in either direction, with nothing lost and no explanation owed to anyone. Housing never depends on anyone's behavior, on any court outcome, or on any reunification: caregiving counts as contribution and it is paid, privacy is maximal within the law and the law's reporting duties are told to families in plain language before they enroll, and if anyone in a home is unsafe, safety overrides every sequence we have. Everyone has a place to call home — and for a family, that means everyone in it.
