### 3.2 Example workflow — a move request (ours, per Amendment B's sequence)

*The sample's workflow (a vendor reports a delay → support team → manager reroutes drivers → a site manager reheats backup product) is a supply-chain reaction. Ours is a household transition, and the governing rule is that the household is never the logistics coordinator of its own move.*

1. **A household asks to move — or the program offers.** The request can be a pocket transfer, a household split, an upgrade, or a **DV safety transfer**, and it can arrive through any channel: the household's case-owner, the pocket coordinator, the grievance line, or the Movement Coordinator directly. **A safety transfer is not queued** (DOC 2 §9.5). Declining movement support costs the household nothing, is recorded nowhere, and changes nothing (Amendment B).
2. **The Movement Coordinator scopes the moving parts.** Household size and unit scale set the band (studio/1BR → 4BR+, Amendment B §budget lines — **framework estimates, quadrupled**, with the verified wage and moving-rate anchors printed underneath). Pets involved → the **Vet/Pet Liaison co-signs** (crate, meds, records confirmed **before the truck rolls**). Children involved → seats and school transfer are handled with the navigator's family-liaison skill, and **children are never unsupervised on move days** (the household's own arrangement, its choice). Medical equipment → handled with the health partner, never improvised.
3. **The pocket coordinator confirms the destination is actually ready.** **Never move a household into an unready unit** — the build-spec checklist runs first (Amendment B error control 1). If the unit is not ready and the household prefers not to wait in it, an interim placement is paid by the program, **pets included**, and **no line is reduced**. The one exception is a DV safety transfer, which brings the unit to standard **in place** rather than making a survivor wait.
4. **Crew and vendor are scheduled.** The coordinator books from the per-pocket vendor list (minimum two movers, at least one cleared for confidential work, each having signed the DOC 1 §6 code) and staffs the **paid participant move-crew** from the published calendar. For a protected address: **unmarked vehicle, no branding, no uniforms, minimum-need crew briefing, confidential scheduling, no return trips required, and the premium is paid without argument** — and the **unbranded safety vehicle goes first**, out of every other commitment.
5. **The move happens, and the money settles behind it.** Fixture order is deliberate — the bed made first, the pet's crate before the human's, the study desk lit, the decompression space real on night one, **connectivity live before the first workday** (a RIGHT, moved with the household). The move-related disbursement goes on the calendar like any other, and the household's case-owner logs the 48-hour confirmation.
6. **Completion is the household's word.** **The move is complete only when the household says it is.** If it went wrong: the grievance channel, and the remedy is **a re-do, not a discount**. The move file holds a date, a vendor, a disbursement, and a confirmation — **a move is not an inspection**, and nothing from a move day flows into anyone's case file.

### 3.3 Escalation, in one line

**Any participant → any staff member or the grievance channel; any staff member → their named report; any reserved decision → the owner.** A breach of the Sacred Protections is escalated **directly to the sponsor's compliance owner, bypassing the person named** (DOC 1 §6) — the org chart bends around that rule, it does not swallow it.

### 3.4 Who speaks for whom

- **Every participant** is a voting member of their cohort council — **one adult, one vote**, no household bloc, no proxy, and in Wave 2+ two elected **participant seats carry votes onto the program board as it forms** (DOC 1 §7, DOC 2 §8.1).
- **Every pocket** has one named Pocket Coordinator who speaks for the pocket relationship — and one named case-owner per participant's payment file, so nobody's file is ownerless.
- **Staff never speak for participants.** No personal narrative, image, or detail appears anywhere public without a separate, specific, revocable written consent for that specific use — and refusing costs nothing, ever (DOC 1 §6.1).
- **The owner speaks for the program's mission** and is the single place a reserved decision lands (§9).

## 12. Quotable close

**The chart, for a household.** An organization chart usually tells you who has power over whom. This one tells you who is accountable to whom, and it is built the way the program is built: nobody carries their own transition alone, and nobody's home depends on a form they filled out correctly. At the top the owner holds the mission and the reserved calls, and is owed the honest thing — a wave gate reported with its miss, not a wave gate reported as a win. Below that, one Program Director carries the caseload cap that keeps a case-owner from becoming a queue, and the gate discipline that keeps a miss from being quietly absorbed. Each pocket has one coordinator who talked to the neighborhood before a single unit was signed. Each participant has **one named human on their payment file**, capped at fifteen people so the name means something — and a case-owner who answers a wrong payment in twenty-four hours and can re-issue it without asking a committee. Paid participant crews carry the couches at a real wage, with a firewall that keeps them from ever deciding anyone's standing; peer mentors support without deciding; an unbranded vehicle waits for the night someone has to leave. Nothing in the chart is filled in: the wages are uncosted, the entity is pending, the funders are unnamed, and not one person has been hired. **That is what an org chart looks like before it is a promise.** What we have built instead is the shape we will hire into, the caps we will not raise, the misses we will publish, and the list of calls that belong to the owner alone. **Everyone has a place to call home — and the people who build it are paid, capped, and answerable.**
