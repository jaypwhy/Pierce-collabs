# Phase-1 pilot: a soft landing in San Diego

This page is a working draft, not a final program. It lays out the Phase-1 pilot concept for San Diego, what a participant experiences across all six pillars, and the owner-directed **Living-in-Development / Living-in-Renovation** track: turn vacant and underused space into dignified, soft-landing-ready homes — and let participants help build them, earning regenerative income along the way. Partners are welcome; budget figures are framework estimates, not verified costs.

## The Phase-1 concept

### Where

San Diego, California — the city where RUHBI starts and the model is proven before it spreads.

### Who

A small cohort of unsheltered San Diegans served first as the proof case — the pilot that proves the universal model for everyone.

### How

One navigable system, RUHBI: housing first, then health, food, hygiene, income, and travel already in place around it.

### What success looks like

Stable homes, growing savings, jobs or businesses started, and a measured case for free housing as normal city infrastructure.

## A participant's experience — all six pillars

One person joins the pilot and receives the whole system, not a list of referrals:

*     
    

### Free Independent Personal Housing

A private home — your own door, your own key, your own kitchen — sponsored at a dignified standard, with no rent and no eviction clock. For many participants this starts through the Living-in-Development / Living-in-Renovation track below.  

*     
    

### Free Universal Health

A sponsored health plan plus same-day access to community clinics, dental care, and mental health support — no co-pay, no paperwork barriers.  

*     
    

### Free Food Resources

Weekly grocery delivery covered by the living stipend, plus food-bank and meal partnerships as backup. Nobody in the pilot ever plans a meal around whether they can afford it.  

*     
    

### Free Hygiene Resources

Every home comes fully set up, and community hygiene partnerships (showers, laundry) are pre-arranged for anyone who needs them.  

*     
    

### Free Regenerative Independent Personal Income

A monthly stipend that covers every need with padding built in, plus the Future Fund entrepreneurial starter grant for participants who want to start a business. Income is designed to exceed monthly outgo so every participant builds their own savings cushion.  

*     
    

### Free Zero Entropy Travel

A prepaid transit pass for the whole household, plus remote-first work options so getting around is a bonus, never a barrier.  

## Living-in-Development / Living-in-Renovation

This is the heart of the housing pillar: vacant and underused space is **acquired**, then **renovated** in a disciplined order, then **occupied with dignity** — with participants earning regenerative income as part of the work crews.

1.    
    

1.  Acquire
    

### Vacant and underused space

The pilot acquires vacant or underused buildings — empty offices, rundown hotels, surplus properties — that would otherwise sit idle. City and county surplus lists, motivated private owners, and partner nonprofits are the target sources.

*     
    

2.  Renovate
    

### Shell to home, in the right order

Each building moves from shell condition to home in a fixed order: secure and safe, then wet rooms (kitchen and bath), then sleep zones, then work zones, then comfort and decor. Two checklists run side by side — see below.

*     
    

3.  Occupy with dignity
    

### Move-in ready, sponsored living

Units are furnished to the sponsored-living CapEx standard (basic to premium, below) and occupied with dignity. Autism accommodations are written into the build spec — not added as extras later.

### Construction & installation checklist (contractor and work-crew)

*   Flooring and finishes (low-sensory, non-toxic materials)
    
*   Paint — predictable, calming palette; no harsh glare surfaces
    
*   Lighting installs — layered, dimmable, flicker-free LEDs; individual controls in every room
    
*   Appliance installation (kitchen and laundry)
    
*   Safety modifications — grab bars, non-slip surfaces, secure locks, fire safety
    
*   Sensory-accommodation builds — sound-dampened walls, quiet zones, predictable room layouts, a decompression (“zen”) space in every unit
    
*   Wet rooms — kitchen and bath brought to code and finished first
    

### Furnishings & decor checklist (the sponsored-living CapEx standard)

*   Kitchenware — cookware, dishes, utensils, small appliances
    
*   Linens and bath — bedding, towels, shower curtains
    
*   Living room — seating, lighting, a table that fits a shared meal
    
*   Bedroom — bed, mattress, storage, blackout-capable window covers
    
*   Office/studio — desk, chair, lamp, reliable connectivity
    
*   Clothing/self — wardrobe basics, outerwear, personal care kit
    
*   Pet setup — bed, bowls, supplies for companions
    

### Renovation timeline — the order that matters

1.  **Secure and safe** — structure, fire safety, security, utilities live.
    
2.  **Wet rooms** — kitchen and bath brought to code and finished.
    
3.  **Sleep zones** — bedrooms ready, quiet, dark-capable.
    
4.  **Work zones** — office/studio spaces with connectivity for remote-first roles.
    
5.  **Comfort and decor** — the modern San Diego aesthetic: light woods, terracotta and clay, a coastal-neutral palette, drought-tolerant outdoor living, indoor-outdoor flow.
    

### Autism accommodations are build requirements, not extras

Sensory-friendly textures and finishes, routine-supporting layouts, predictable and controllable lighting, and a decompression (“zen”) space in every unit are written into the construction checklist itself — every home gets them, from day one.

### Wellness animals are family — accommodations are requirements, not extras

Companion animals are family. Pet-friendly housing is an **acquisition and renovation requirement** of this model — the shelter pet-exclusion barriers that force people to choose between a roof and their animal end here. No participant gives up a companion animal to join the pilot.

*   **Pet Setup support (one-time CapEx): $2,000–$6,000** — bed, bowls, crate, leash and carrier, food starter supply, vet intake visit. Framework estimate from the Pierce sponsored-living budget, labeled as an estimate.
    
*   **Pet Care Stipend: $600–$1,200/month** — part of the variable living stipend, covering food, vet care, and supplies, flexed to the animal's needs. Framework estimate, labeled as an estimate.
    
*   **Settling-in padding for people AND animals** — animal transport on move-in day, acclimation time built into the move schedule, predictable feeding and walking routines from day one, and decompression space in every unit (aligned with the autism-accommodation standards above, which calm every nervous system — human and animal).
    
*   Accommodations **flex to every participant's circumstance** — different species, sizes, ages, medical needs, and bonded pairs are planned for individually, the way every part of the living stipend flexes.

## Owner-directed standing terms for the pilot

### Future Fund entrepreneurial starter grant

**$1,000,000 or more per participant** to start and grow a business — the full-vision figure, with headroom kept above $1M. At 100 participants that is $100M+ in grant capital; the near-term path is **phased funding**, starting with a small cohort. Labeled an aspirational full-vision figure, not a committed budget.

### Remote-first positions

All pilot positions — agents and consultants, participant work roles — are remote-friendly. A home anywhere is a valid work base, and zero-entropy travel stays a bonus, not a requirement.

### Flexible living stipend

The stipend is a **range that flexes to individual circumstance**, not a fixed amount — set within the operating scenarios above (lean to premium) based on each participant's household needs.

### Budget philosophy: fluid with padding — lagniappe

Every daily, weekly, and monthly need is covered **with a bit extra built in** — the “lagniappe” that turns a tight budget into a cushion. Assistance is available whenever needed, and regenerative income ( $26,000+ lean to $40,000+ premium per month) is designed to **exceed monthly outgo** (≈$16,800–$27,600), so each participant builds their own savings cushion and the surplus rolls forward continuously. The padding is **generational** — sized so housing instability never repeats, for this generation or the next. Figures remain framework estimates.
