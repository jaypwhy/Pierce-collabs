The evidence base

# The Studies

Five studies and programs behind Project Home Key — what each one tested, what it found, and honestly what it did and didn't prove. Every key number has a source link; estimates are labeled as estimates.

> When people are given a safe, permanent home with no strings attached, they overwhelmingly stay housed — retention of 73–88% in the strongest trials, including for people with severe mental illness and addictions (Pathways 2000; At Home/Chez Soi 2014; JECH 2019). In Finland, making this the national approach cut long-term homelessness by two-thirds over fourteen years, while saving society at least €15,000 per housed person per year in other costs (Y-Foundation 2023; OECD 2019). And when people are given dependable money with no strings attached, they do not stop working; they work at least as much, their income becomes less volatile, their mental health improves, and local economies grow — effects seen in Stockton (2021, 2023), Kenya's 295-village UBI experiment (2024), and meta-analyses of 114 cash-transfer programs (2024). The evidence does not show that housing alone heals people, nor that income alone creates housing — it shows that _unconditional housing and unconditional income are safe, effective, cost-saving infrastructure_. That is the foundation Project Home Key's universal model stands on: free shelter and dependable income are not risky experiments; they are the evidence-backed baseline. (Sources: Tsemberis & Eisenberg 2000; MHCC 2014; JECH 2019; Y-Foundation 2023; OECD 2019; SEED/JUH 2023; GiveDirectly/IPA 2024; NBER 2024.)

Stockton, California · 2019–2021

## Stockton SEED — the U.S. guaranteed-income pilot

The Stockton Economic Empowerment Demonstration (SEED) gave 125 residents of low-income Stockton neighborhoods $500 per month, no strings attached, for 24 months (February 2019 – January 2021).

It was a randomized controlled trial funded entirely by philanthropy (about $3 million), led by then-Mayor Michael Tubbs, and evaluated by independent researchers.

### Key numbers

- 125 recipients · $500/month · 24 months · no work requirements. [Source: SEED press release, 2021-03-03](https://www.stocktondemonstration.org/press-landing/guaranteed-income-increases-employment-improves-financial-and-physical-health)
- First-year employment: full-time employment rose from 28% to 40% of recipients, vs. 32% to 37% in the control group. [Source: SEED press release, 2021-03-03](https://www.stocktondemonstration.org/press-landing/guaranteed-income-increases-employment-improves-financial-and-physical-health)
- Income volatility cut (19% vs. 26% swings, p=0.039); Kessler-10 distress moved from “likely mild mental health disorder” to “likely well” in year one; far more recipients could cover an unexpected $400 expense (p<0.001). [Source: Journal of Urban Health, 2023 (peer-reviewed)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11524-023-00723-0)
- Honest limits: small sample, heavy attrition, and the final peer-reviewed employment gains were not statistically significant; most benefits faded during COVID and after payments ended. [Source: CalMatters, April 2023](https://calmatters.org/california-divide/2023/04/california-guaranteed-income/)

### What it proves for Project Home Key

- Unconditional money does not discourage work.
- Guaranteed income calms volatility and lifts mental health while paid — supports regenerative-income design.
- Reliable income is infrastructure, not charity.

### What it does NOT prove

- Job creation (not significant in the final paper).
- Lasting effects after payments stop.
- Anything about housing — or generalization beyond one small city.

Finland · 2008–present (the norm story)

## Finland Housing First — from pilot to national policy

Finland made Housing First national policy (PAAVO I 2008–2011, PAAVO II 2012–2015), replaced shelters with permanent rental homes, and kept going.

The Y-Foundation became a national landlord in service of the goal — today Finland's 4th-largest landlord, with about 18,000 apartments and some 12,000 people housed since 1987.

### Key numbers

- Long-term homelessness fell 35% from 2008 to 2015; by 2015 fewer than 7,000 lived alone without a home. [Source: Y-Foundation, “Home for All,” 2022](https://ysaatio.fi/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Home-for-all.pdf)
- 2008 to 2022: homeless living alone down 54%; long-term homeless down 68% (3,686 homeless living alone; 1,133 long-term homeless in 2022). [Source: Y-Foundation “Kotiin / Home,” April 2023](https://ysaatio.fi/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Kotiin_Home-Kaakinen-2023-en-final.pdf)
- 7,290 homes provided in 10 years; housing retention around 80%. [Source: OECD, “Housing First: How Finland is Ending Homelessness,” 2019](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2019/05/housing-first-how-finland-is-ending-homelessness_c75b7e47/aa39e88f-en.pdf)
- Housing one homeless person with support saves society at least €15,000 per person per year (2011 Tampere University study, minimum estimate); Helsinki prevention estimates €17,000–€52,000. [Source: Y-Foundation, “A Home of Your Own,” 2023](https://ysaatio.fi/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/A_Home_of_Your_Own_lowres_spreads.pdf)

### What it proves for Project Home Key

- The norm thesis directly: pilot → national policy → 15-year decline.
- Housing people is cheaper than managing homelessness.
- Condition-free housing works for the hardest cases.

### What it does NOT prove

- 1:1 transfer to San Diego (small, wealthy country with decades of social housing).
- The €15,000 figure is a 2011 estimate — treat it as an estimate.
- Finland provides subsidized/social housing, not universal free housing with income — RUHBI's income pillar goes beyond what Finland proved.

Berlin, Germany · ongoing

## Berlin “Wohnen für Hilfe” — non-monetary exchange housing

Home-sharing: someone with a spare room (usually a senior) offers it rent-free or cheap to someone who needs housing (usually a student) in exchange for agreed everyday help — shopping, gardening, company.

No rent money changes hands; the tenant pays only utilities, and medical or nursing care is excluded. Run in Berlin by Sonay Soziales Leben e.V., funded by the Berlin Senate, with free mediation.

### Key numbers

- Exchange rule: 1 hour of help per month per m² of room (a 15 m² room ≈ 15 hours/month). [Source: Deutsches Studierendenwerk](https://www.studierendenwerke.de/en/topics/accomodation/tips-for-finding-accommodation/wohnen-fuer-hilfe-living-for-help)
- Germany-wide scale: 34 active sites; 4,249 partnerships since program start; 272 new in 2021; about 221 active at end of 2021 (20 of 34 cities reporting). [Source: Bundestag Berichtsvorlage Nr. 2379/X, 2022](https://buendnis-fuer-menschenwuerde-und-arbeit.de/images/pdf/spd-2379_x_berichtsvorlage.pdf)
- Berlin structure: free mediation, guided meet-and-greet, sublease contract, no fixed age limit. [Source: Wohnen für Hilfe Berlin](https://www.xn--wohnen-fr-hilfe-berlin-zlc.de/)

### What it proves for Project Home Key

- Non-monetary exchange housing exists at scale and is government-funded-normal.
- Activates unused space without construction.
- Addresses loneliness alongside housing.

### What it does NOT prove

- A path for unsheltered people (most partnerships are students).
- Scale beyond thousands.
- Any rigorous outcomes — proof of concept, not a controlled study.

Kenya · 2017–present

## GiveDirectly Kenya — the world's longest UBI experiment

The world's largest and longest UBI experiment (about $30M, ~295 villages, ~73,511 people) with four arms: 12-year monthly UBI (~$22.50/month), 2-year monthly payments, a one-time ~$500 lump sum, and a control group.

Results after two years, published with Innovations for Poverty Action in November 2024, compared what lump sums vs. ongoing streams each do best.

### Key numbers

- Design: 295 villages; about 15,189 households across four arms. [Source: IPA working paper, Nov 2024](https://poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/2024-11/UBI_main_paper.pdf)
- After 2 years of long-term UBI: businesses +14%, revenues +41%, net revenues +52%; labor supply did not fall (a shift toward self-employment). [Source: IPA working paper, Nov 2024](https://poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/2024-11/UBI_main_paper.pdf)
- Lump-sum gains similar to or larger than 12-year UBI; short-term monthly streams underperformed both; mental health improved more in monthly-stream arms; no inflation (0.1% price effect). [Source: GiveDirectly 2023 UBI results](https://www.givedirectly.org/2023-ubi-results)
- Meta-analysis of 114 studies across 72 programs: strong positive effects on 10 of 13 outcomes, with no dependency effect. [Source: NBER Working Paper 32779, 2024](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32779/revisions/w32779.rev1.pdf)

### What it proves for Project Home Key

- Design lesson for the Future Fund: combine a lump sum with an ongoing stream — lump sums drive investment, streams protect wellbeing.
- Dependable long-term income changes behavior; short-term-only income underperforms.
- Three evidence tiers agree: do not fear reduced work.

### What it does NOT prove

- Anything about housing.
- Automatic transfer to a U.S. city.
- That $1M-scale grants behave proportionally — Kenya transfers are tiny by comparison, and no study tests grants that large. “$1M works even better” is a projection, labeled as such.

United States & Canada · 2000–2020

## Housing First trials — the strongest peer-reviewed findings

Housing First gives people a permanent home with no preconditions — no sobriety, treatment, or employment requirements — plus voluntary support services.

It has been tested in randomized trials from New York's Pathways program to Canada's five-city, 2,000+ person At Home/Chez Soi study: the most replicated result in homelessness research.

### Key numbers

- Pathways (New York): 88% retained housing over 5 years vs. 47% in traditional programs. [Source: Tsemberis & Eisenberg, Psychiatric Services, 2000](https://ps.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ps.51.4.487)
- Pathways RCT (225 people, 24 months): housed faster, stayed housed more, more choice — with no worsening of substance use or symptoms. [Source: Tsemberis et al., AJPH, 2004](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1448313/)
- At Home/Chez Soi (5 cities, 2,000+ people): 62% housed all of the time in the final 6 months vs. 31%; $9.60–$21.72 saved per $10 invested (up to $21.72 for the highest-cost 10%). [Source: Mental Health Commission of Canada, 2014](https://www.mentalhealthcommission.ca/wp-content/uploads/drupal/mhcc_at_home_report_national_cross-site_eng_2_0.pdf)
- Systematic review (4 RCTs): 2.46x more likely stably housed at 18–24 months; fewer emergency-department visits (0.63x); no substance-use increase — caveat: all trials rated high risk of bias. [Source: JECH, 2019](https://jech.bmj.com/content/73/5/379)
- Community Guide review: median 41% greater housing stability; 88% greater decrease in homelessness. [Source: The Community Guide](https://thecommunityguide.org/findings/social-determinants-health-housing-first-programs.html)

### What it proves for Project Home Key

- Housing First keeps the “hardest to house” housed — the strongest, most-replicated result in the field.
- It is cost-effective: crisis-service savings offset much or all of the cost.
- Condition-free, dignity-first housing is the evidence-backed default.

### What it does NOT prove

- Improved mental health, substance use, or employment — housing alone is not treatment. This is the honest limit.
- RUHBI's housing-plus-income combination is our design hypothesis: each component is backed, but the package is untested.
- Most trials run about 2 years, and they used existing rental stock via supplements — not free, not built or renovated, not universal.

## Reading the evidence honestly

Two layers: what the evidence firmly supports, and what to handle with care. Green means strong; amber means label it carefully.

### Strong evidence

- Housing First keeps people housed far better than treatment-first (2.5x at 18–24 months; retention 62–88%).
- Housing First reduces crisis-service use — cost-neutral to cost-saving (Canada; Finland).
- Unconditional cash does not reduce work, and improves stability, mental health, and local economies.
- Finland proves policy-scale decline is achievable.
- Lump sums drive investment, streams protect wellbeing; short-term-only income underperforms both.
- Housing alone does NOT reliably improve mental-health, substance-use, or employment outcomes (a negative finding — and a strong one).

### Handle with care

- “Cash increases employment” — not statistically significant in the final Stockton paper.
- “Saves money everywhere, always” — savings vary by group and era.
- “$1M grants produce proportional results” — a projection; no study tests that scale.
- “The RUHBI combination works” — a design hypothesis, untested as a package.
- “Finland is the only country where homelessness is falling” — widely reported; verify before quoting.
