## 1. Stockton SEED — the U.S. guaranteed-income pilot (Stockton, California)

**What it was:** The Stockton Economic Empowerment Demonstration (SEED) gave **125 residents of low-income Stockton neighborhoods $500 per month, no strings attached, for 24 months** (February 2019 – January 2021). It was a randomized controlled trial (RCT) funded entirely by philanthropy (~$3 million), led by then-Mayor Michael Tubbs, and evaluated by independent researchers.

**Key numbers:**
- **125 recipients, $500/month, 24 months, no work requirements.** Source: SEED press release, 2021-03-03 — https://www.stocktondemonstration.org/press-landing/guaranteed-income-increases-employment-improves-financial-and-physical-health (also NPR, 2021-03-04 — https://www.npr.org/2021/03/04/973653719/california-program-giving-500-no-strings-attached-stipends-pays-off-study-finds)
- **First-year employment:** full-time employment rose from 28% to 40% of recipients, vs. 32% to 37% in the control group. Source: SEED press release, 2021-03-03.
- **Income volatility cut:** month-to-month income swings 19% vs. 26% control (p=0.039). **Mental health:** Kessler-10 distress moved from "likely mild mental health disorder" to "likely well" in year one. **Financial cushion:** far more recipients could cover an unexpected $400 expense (p<0.001). Source: peer-reviewed final analysis, *Journal of Urban Health*, 2023 — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11524-023-00723-0
- **Critiques:** small sample, heavy attrition (~35% controls / ~55% recipients reachable at end); the final peer-reviewed employment gains were **not** statistically significant; most benefits faded during COVID and after payments ended. Sources: CalMatters, April 2023 — https://calmatters.org/california-divide/2023/04/california-guaranteed-income/ ; https://www.evidencebasedpolicy.org/study-reviews-1/uncondit'l-cash-stockton

**Proves for PHK:** unconditional money does not discourage work; guaranteed income calms volatility and lifts mental health *while paid* — supports regenerative-income design; reliable income is infrastructure, not charity.
**Does NOT prove:** job creation (not significant in final paper); lasting effects after payments stop; anything about housing; generalization beyond one small city.

## 2. Finland Housing First — from pilot to national policy (the norm story)

**What it was:** Finland made Housing First national policy (PAAVO I 2008–2011, PAAVO II 2012–2015), replaced shelters with permanent rental homes, and kept going. The Y-Foundation became a national landlord in service of the goal.

**Key numbers:**
- **Long-term homelessness fell 35% from 2008 to 2015**; by 2015 fewer than 7,000 lived alone without a home. Source: Y-Foundation, "Home for All," 2022 — https://ysaatio.fi/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Home-for-all.pdf
- **2008 to 2022: homeless living alone down 54%; long-term homeless down 68%.** 2022 counts: 3,686 homeless living alone; 1,133 long-term homeless. Source: Y-Foundation "Kotiin / Home" (Kaakinen), April 2023 — https://ysaatio.fi/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Kotiin_Home-Kaakinen-2023-en-final.pdf
- **7,290 homes provided in 10 years.** Source: OECD, "Housing First: How Finland is Ending Homelessness," 2019 — https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2019/05/housing-first-how-finland-is-ending-homelessness_c75b7e47/aa39e88f-en.pdf
- **Retention ~80%** (international average incl. Finland). Source: Y-Foundation 2023 "Kotiin."
- **Cost:** housing one homeless person with support **saves society at least €15,000/person/year** (2011 Tampere University study, minimum estimate); Helsinki prevention estimates €17,000–€52,000; Tampere's Härmälä unit ~€250,000 saved in one year. Sources: Y-Foundation 2023 "Kotiin"; "A Home of Your Own," 2023 — https://ysaatio.fi/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/A_Home_of_Your_Own_lowres_spreads.pdf
- **Scale:** Y-Foundation is Finland's 4th-largest landlord — 18,000 apartments, ~26,600 residents, ~12,000 housed since 1987. Source: Y-Foundation 2022.

**Proves:** the norm thesis directly (pilot → national policy → 15-year decline); housing people is cheaper than managing homelessness; condition-free housing works for the hardest cases.
**Does NOT prove:** 1:1 transfer to San Diego (small, wealthy, homogeneous country with decades of social housing); the €15,000 figure is a 2011 estimate; Finland provides subsidized/social housing, NOT universal free housing with income — RUHBI's income pillar goes beyond what Finland proved. "Only country where homelessness is falling" = widely reported, verify inside the OECD PDF before quoting.

## 3. Berlin "Wohnen für Hilfe" — non-monetary exchange housing

**What it was:** Home-sharing: someone with a spare room (usually a senior) offers it rent-free or cheap to someone who needs housing (usually a student) **in exchange for agreed everyday help** (shopping, gardening, company). No rent money; tenant pays only utilities; medical/nursing care excluded. Run in Berlin by Sonay Soziales Leben e.V., funded by the Berlin Senate; free mediation.

**Key numbers:**
- **Exchange rule:** 1 hour of help per month per m² of room (15 m² = ~15 h/month). Sources: Deutsches Studierendenwerk — https://www.studierendenwerke.de/en/topics/accomodation/tips-for-finding-accommodation/wohnen-fuer-hilfe-living-for-help ; https://www.stwwb.de/en/housing/homesharing
- **Germany scale:** 34 active sites; **4,249 partnerships since program start; 272 new in 2021; ~221 active end of 2021** (20 of 34 cities reporting). Source: Bundestag Berichtsvorlage Nr. 2379/X, 2022 — https://buendnis-fuer-menschenwuerde-und-arbeit.de/images/pdf/spd-2379_x_berichtsvorlage.pdf
- **Berlin structure:** free mediation, guided meet-and-greet, sublease contract, no fixed age limit. Source: https://www.xn--wohnen-fr-hilfe-berlin-zlc.de/
- **Addendum session (2026-09-05, researcher-2):** Berlin coordination center launch date **confirmed June 26, 2026** (Berlin Senate press release). Berlin-specific tandem counts: checked, currently unpublished. Housing First fidelity-scale citations: located in-session but not yet written out with URLs — still needs a citable link before site use. UNHCR / Bolsa Familia / CERB entries: resolved in-session, citations not yet captured. FEMA IA: not verified (search cap).

**Proves:** non-monetary exchange housing exists at scale and is government-funded-normal; activates unused space without construction; addresses loneliness.
**Does NOT prove:** a path for unsheltered people (most partnerships are students); scale beyond thousands; any rigorous outcome studies — proof of concept, not controlled study.

## 4. GiveDirectly — Kenya long-term UBI + the cash-transfer evidence base

**What it was:** The world's largest/longest UBI experiment (2017–, ~$30M, ~295 villages, ~73,511 people). Four arms: 12-year monthly UBI (~$22.50/mo), 2-year monthly, one-time ~$500 lump sum, control.

**Key numbers:**
- **Design:** 295 villages; ~15,189 households. Source: "Universal Basic Income: Short-Term Results from a Long-Term Experiment in Kenya," IPA, Nov 2024 — https://poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/2024-11/UBI_main_paper.pdf ; https://www.givedirectly.org/ubi
- **After 2 years (long-term UBI):** businesses +14%, revenues +41%, net revenues +52%; **labor supply did not fall** (shift to self-employment). Source: same paper.
- **Lump sum vs. monthly:** lump-sum gains similar or larger than 12-year UBI; short-term monthly streams underperformed both; **mental health improved MORE in monthly-stream arms** (lump-sum recipients "feel the weight of the future"); no inflation (0.1% price effect). Sources: Nov 2024 paper; https://www.givedirectly.org/2023-ubi-results
- **Meta-analyses:** 114 studies / 72 programs — strong positive effects on 10 of 13 outcomes, **no dependency effect** (NBER WP 32779, 2024 — https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32779/revisions/w32779.rev1.pdf); wellbeing meta-analysis: 45 studies, 116,999 people, small significant wellbeing boost (d=0.13) (McGuire et al., Nature Human Behaviour, 2022).
- **Modeled estimate (label as such):** GiveDirectly $1,000 lump sum ≈ 2.10 WELLBYs — https://www.happierlivesinstitute.org/givedirectly-comprehensive-summary/

**Design lessons for the Future Fund ($250K+ per participant):** combine lump sum + ongoing stream (lump sums drive investment; streams protect wellbeing; the combination matches the evidence); commitment length matters ( dependable long-term income changes behavior); do not fear reduced work (three evidence tiers agree); pair a one-time grant with durable monthly income to avoid the "weight of the future." **Honest caveat:** Kenya transfers are tiny vs. $250K; no study tests grants that large — "250K works even better" is a projection, label it.
**Does NOT prove:** anything about housing; effects don't auto-transfer to a U.S. city.

## 5. Housing First evidence (U.S. and Canada) — the strongest peer-reviewed findings

**Key numbers:**
- **Pathways NY:** 88% retained housing over 5 years vs. 47% traditional (Tsemberis & Eisenberg, *Psychiatric Services*, 2000 — https://ps.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ps.51.4.487)
- **Pathways RCT (225 people, 24 months):** housed faster, stayed housed more, more choice, **no worsening** of substance use or symptoms (Tsemberis et al., *AJPH*, 2004 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1448313/)
- **At Home/Chez Soi (5 cities, ~2,000+):** 62% housed all of the time in final 6 months vs. 31%; 73% of days stably housed vs. 32%; costs $14,177–$22,257/person/yr; **$9.60–21.72 saved per $10 invested** (up to $21.72 for highest-cost 10%) (MHCC final report 2014 — https://www.mentalhealthcommission.ca/wp-content/uploads/drupal/mhcc_at_home_report_national_cross-site_eng_2_0.pdf ; JAMA Netw Open 2020 — https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2019.9782)
- **Systematic review (4 RCTs):** 2.46x more likely stably housed at 18–24 months; fewer ED visits (0.63x); no substance-use increase; caveat: all trials rated high risk of bias (JECH 2019 — https://jech.bmj.com/content/73/5/379)
- **Community Guide:** median 41% greater housing stability; 88% greater decrease in homelessness (https://thecommunityguide.org/findings/social-determinants-health-housing-first-programs.html)
- **Fidelity:** needs a citable URL before it goes on the site (search cap hit).

**Proves:** HF keeps the "hardest to house" housed (strongest, most-replicated result in the field); cost-effective; condition-free dignity-first housing is the evidence-backed default.
**Does NOT prove:** improved mental health/substance use/employment (housing alone is not treatment — the honest limit: RUHBI's housing+income combination is OUR design hypothesis, components are each backed); mostly 2-year trials; existing rental stock via supplements — not free, not built/renovated, not universal.

## What the evidence base supports (site-quotable paragraph)

> When people are given a safe, permanent home with no strings attached, they overwhelmingly stay housed — retention of 73–88% in the strongest trials, including for people with severe mental illness and addictions (Pathways 2000; At Home/Chez Soi 2014; JECH 2019). In Finland, making this the national approach cut long-term homelessness by two-thirds over fourteen years, while saving society at least €15,000 per housed person per year in other costs (Y-Foundation 2023; OECD 2019). And when people are given dependable money with no strings attached, they do not stop working; they work at least as much, their income becomes less volatile, their mental health improves, and local economies grow — effects seen in Stockton (2021, 2023), Kenya's 295-village UBI experiment (2024), and meta-analyses of 114 cash-transfer programs (2024). The evidence does not show that housing alone heals people, nor that income alone creates housing — it shows that *unconditional housing and unconditional income are safe, effective, cost-saving infrastructure*. That is the foundation Project Home Key's universal model stands on: free shelter and dependable income are not risky experiments; they are the evidence-backed baseline. (Sources: Tsemberis & Eisenberg 2000; MHCC 2014; JECH 2019; Y-Foundation 2023; OECD 2019; SEED/JUH 2023; GiveDirectly/IPA 2024; NBER 2024.)

## Strong vs. weak evidence (honesty layer)

**STRONG:** HF keeps people housed far better than treatment-first (2.5x at 18–24 months; retention 62–88%); HF reduces crisis-service use, cost-neutral-to-saving (Canada; Finland); unconditional cash does not reduce work and improves stability/mental health/local economies; Finland proves policy-scale decline is achievable; lump sums drive investment, streams protect wellbeing, short-term-only underperforms both; housing alone does NOT reliably improve mental-health/substance/employment outcomes (negative finding, strong).

**WEAK / LABEL-CAREFULLY:** "cash increases employment" (not significant in final paper); "saves money everywhere always" (varies by group/era); "$250K grants produce proportional results" (projection, no study tests that scale); "RUHBI combination works" (design hypothesis, untested as a package); Berlin tandem counts + coordination-center launch date (unverified); "Finland is the only country where homelessness is falling" (widely reported; verify inside OECD PDF before printing).

## 4a. Brazil Bolsa Familia
- Conditional cash program for poor families; monthly payment continues while kids stay in school and get health checkups. Reaches about 21 million families - roughly one-quarter of Brazil's population - with a minimum benefit of about $120/month. A related study found it prevented more than 8.2 million hospitalizations and 713,083 deaths between 2004 and 2019 (Lancet Public Health family of reporting). Date checked 2026-09-06.

## 4b. Canada CERB
- Canada's emergency COVID income benefit for workers who lost income; ended September 2020, transitioning recipients to expanded Employment Insurance. Paid out $81.64 billion to 8.9 million recipients (press-reported total, not a direct canada.ca table - label "reported figure"). Date checked 2026-09-06.
