The current system often forces people into shelters as a gatekeeper for Housing First or other housing programs, which can be problematic for those who are already experiencing homelessness but may not want to (or can’t) stay in a shelter due to safety, health, or personal reasons. Let’s refine the approach to focus on **direct pathways to housing and income without requiring shelter stays**, where possible, while still addressing the structural barriers.

* * *

### **Revised Strategy: Avoiding Shelter as a Prerequisite**

The goal is to **identify and leverage programs that bypass shelter stays** or offer alternatives for those who cannot or do not want to stay in shelters. Here’s how to structure this:

* * *

### **1. Direct Housing First Programs (No Shelter Required)**

Some Housing First programs **do not require shelter stays**—they accept referrals from other sources (e.g., hospitals, mutual aid, or direct applications). Here’s how to find them:

#### **A. Housing First Programs with Alternative Referral Pathways**

*   **Pathways to Housing (NYC)**:
    *   While Pathways typically requires a shelter stay, they also accept referrals from **hospitals, mutual aid groups, or legal aid organizations**. If you’re connected through one of these, you may bypass the shelter requirement.
        
    *   [Pathways to Housing Referral Form](https://www.pathwaystohousing.org/referral-form/) (check if they accept non-shelter referrals).
        
*   **The Bridge Housing (LA)**:
    *   Primarily serves shelter residents, but they may accept referrals from **mutual aid networks, hospitals, or legal aid** if you can demonstrate extreme need.
        
    *   [The Bridge Housing Contact](https://www.thebridgehousing.org/contact/).
        
*   **The Way Home (Chicago)**:
    *   Focuses on Housing First but may accept referrals from **mutual aid groups or hospitals** if you can’t access shelter.
        
    *   [The Way Home Referral](https://www.thewayhomechicago.org/referrals/).
        

#### **B. Programs That Accept Direct Applications**

*   **Project Home (Boston)**:
    *   A Housing First program that accepts **direct applications** from individuals experiencing homelessness, including those not in shelters.
        
    *   [Project Home Application](https://www.projecthome.org/).
        
*   **Street to Home (Seattle)**:
    *   Offers Housing First with **direct intake** for those in immediate need, including those not in shelters.
        
    *   [Street to Home Contact](https://www.streettohome.org/).
        
*   **Local Nonprofits with Direct Intake**:
    *   Many smaller nonprofits or mutual aid groups run Housing First programs. Search for **"Housing First [Your City]"** and look for organizations with **direct application forms** or **no shelter requirement**.
        

* * *

### **2. Mutual Aid + Housing Combo (No Shelter Required)**

Mutual aid networks can **connect you directly to housing** without requiring a shelter stay. Here’s how:

#### **A. Mutual Aid Groups Offering Housing**

*   **NYC Mutual Aid**:
    *   Offers **emergency housing referrals** directly to individuals, bypassing shelters.
        
    *   [NYC Mutual Aid Housing Referrals](https://nycmutualaid.org/housing/).
        
*   **Portland Mutual Aid**:
    *   Provides **direct housing placements** through their network.
        
    *   [Portland Mutual Aid Housing](https://portlandmutualaid.org/housing/).
        
*   **Local Facebook Groups**:
    *   Search for **"[Your City] Mutual Aid Housing"** or **"[Your City] Housing First"**—many groups offer **direct housing placements** without shelter requirements.
        

#### **B. Pop-Up Shelters & Safe Spaces**

*   **Mutual Aid Hub** ([mutualaidhub.org](http://mutualaidhub.org)) lists **pop-up shelters and safe spaces** that may not require long-term stays.
    
*   **Example**: [LA Mutual Aid](https://lamutualaid.org/) offers **short-term housing** for those in crisis.
    

* * *

### **3. Hospital Diversion + Direct Housing Placement**

If you’re in a hospital, **ask for a diversion worker** to connect you directly to housing **without requiring a shelter stay**. Many hospitals have **Housing First partnerships** that bypass shelters.

#### **A. Hospital Diversion Programs**

*   **NYC’s Hospital Diversion Program**:
    *   Connects patients to **direct housing placements** (e.g., Pathways to Housing, The Bridge).
        
    *   [NYC Hospital Diversion](https://www1.nyc.gov/assets/doh/downloads/pdf/health/hospital_diversion.pdf).
        
*   **LA’s Hospital Diversion**:
    *   Directs patients to **Housing First programs** (e.g., The Bridge Housing).
        
    *   [LA Hospital Diversion](https://www.lacounty.gov/hha/).
        

#### **B. Veterans’ Direct Housing**

*   **VA’s HUD-VASH Program**:
    *   **No shelter requirement**—direct housing placements for veterans.
        
    *   [VA HUD-VASH](https://www.va.gov/homeless/VASSH.asp).
        

* * *

### **4. Legal & ID Workarounds (No Shelter Required)**

If lack of ID or legal status is blocking housing, **legal aid and mutual aid can help bypass shelter requirements**:

#### **A. Free ID & Legal Support**

*   **American Citizenship Project**:
    *   Provides **free ID for homeless individuals** (no shelter required).
        
    *   [American Citizenship Project](https://www.americancitizenshipproject.org/).
        
*   **Local Legal Aid**:
    *   Many legal aid organizations offer **free ID/birth certificate assistance** for housing applications.
        
    *   [Legal Services Corporation](https://www.lsc.gov/).
        

#### **B. Mutual Aid for ID & Housing**

*   Some mutual aid groups (e.g., **NYC Mutual Aid, Portland Mutual Aid**) offer **ID assistance + housing referrals** directly.
    

* * *

### **5. Public Housing Workarounds (No Shelter Required)**

While Section 8 waitlists are long, some **preference points or alternative programs** can help:

#### **A. Preference Points for Public Housing**

*   **Domestic Violence Survivors**: Many PHAs give **priority points** for survivors.
    
*   **Veterans**: **100% preference** in public housing.
    
*   **Disabled Individuals**: Some PHAs offer **priority placement**.
    

#### **B. Alternative Public Housing Programs**

*   **Section 811 (Supportive Housing for Disabled)**:
    *   **No shelter requirement**—direct applications accepted.
        
    *   [HUD Section 811](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/hsg/sfh/section811).
        
*   **Project-Based Section 8 (Direct Placement)**:
    *   Some landlords accept **direct Section 8 vouchers** without shelter stays.
        

* * *

### **Revised Example Journey: Avoiding Shelter as a Prerequisite**

Here’s a **zero-shelter pathway** to housing and income:

1.  **Step 1: Connect to Mutual Aid or Hospital Diversion**
    *   If injured/ill: Ask for **hospital diversion** → direct housing referral.
        
    *   If not in a hospital: Reach out to **mutual aid groups** (e.g., NYC Mutual Aid) for **direct housing referrals**.
        
2.  **Step 2: Apply to Housing First Programs with Alternative Intake**
    *   Use **mutual aid or hospital connections** to bypass shelter requirements.
        
    *   Example: **Project Home (Boston)** accepts direct applications.
        
3.  **Step 3: Apply for Public Housing (Section 8 or PHA)**
    *   Apply directly to **PHA** (e.g., NYC Housing Authority) for **priority points** (if eligible).
        
    *   If on a long waitlist, apply for **Section 811** (for disabled individuals).
        
4.  **Step 4: Get Unconditional Cash (SNAP, Mutual Aid, etc.)**
    *   Apply for **SNAP** (food stamps) via [Benefits.gov](http://Benefits.gov).
        
    *   Check **local mutual aid** for **one-time cash assistance**.
        
5.  **Step 5: Legal & ID Support (If Needed)**
    *   Use **American Citizenship Project** or **legal aid** to get ID/birth certificate.
        
    *   Some mutual aid groups (e.g., NYC Mutual Aid) offer **ID + housing referrals**.
        
6.  **Step 6: Long-Term Stability (Housing + Income Combo)**
    *   If eligible, apply for **RUHBI-style grants** (e.g., Habitat for Humanity’s "Sweat Equity").
        
    *   If in a city with **Housing First + cash programs**, combine both for stability.

### **Key Takeaways: Zero-Shelter Pathway**

| **Step** | **Action** | **Timeframe** | **Upfront Cost** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. **Connect to Mutual Aid/Hospital Diversion** | Reach out to mutual aid or ask for hospital diversion | **Same day** | $0 |
| 2. **Apply to Housing First (Alternative Intake)** | Use mutual aid/hospital connections to bypass shelter | 1–4 weeks | $0 |
| 3. **Apply for Public Housing (Section 8/811)** | Direct application to PHA (priority points if eligible) | 6–24 months | $0 |
