# **Morbraii — business plan (revision 14, working)**

_(revision 13 → 14: the owner's new game_ **_Morbraii Mission — Acorn Tap_** _is built and verified by me in both modes, touch-first for phones; and the business name is corrected to_ **_Morbraii_** _everywhere it is shown — the one-i spelling was the typo.)_

## **What we're building**

Morbraii is **one message that any available channel can carry**. A letter or phrase is encoded once, then expressed through whichever channels the person still has: **timed vibration**, **light**, **tone**, or the **2×3 touch cell**. The codes are **Morse** (timed) and **Braille** (touch cells), bridged by a **visual hybrid glyph** that draws both at once, plus a **tactile-only font** for clean 1:1 interchange. The owner's goal: help people across **many situations** with **one definite communication solution that can transcend movement** — the code survives losing a channel (no vibration, no light, no hearing, no speech, no hand movement) instead of being tied to the one device you happen to hold. **Timing (owner): two profiles, both real.**

*   **Standard Morse (ITU)** — the true 1 : 3 : 1 : 3 : 7 unit grid at 5 / 8 / 12 wpm. What a learner acquires here reads on any real Morse channel.
    
*   **Morbraii spacing** — letters identical to standard (dot 1 unit, dash 3) with exaggerated boundaries (element 2 / letter 6 / word 14) so they can be felt, labelled on the site as ours and **not** ITU.
    

**Channels and representations (owner):**

*   **Tone — two live deliveries.** _Discrete pings_ ("on/off keying · standard") and a _sustained carrier_ ("ours · not ITU"): one tone that never goes silent, swelling while a dot or dash sounds and dipping in the gaps, so a boundary is heard as a drop in energy rather than silence. Same timing model and pitch map; waveform, register, per-user hearing ceiling and real Hz shown.
    
*   **Light** — the same rhythm with selectable colour sets.
    
*   **Vibration** — honest limit: the web Vibration API is on/off only. Frequency-tuned haptics need native access to the phone's actuator. We do not claim otherwise.
    
*   **Manual alphabet (live)** — A–Z **ASL one-handed fingerspelling** as SVG line art, beside the Morse wave, the Braille cell and the A–Z reference. Handshapes verified against cited references; **BSL is two-handed and is not shown**; **J and Z are motion letters** and the site says so; **nothing reads your hand**. Four drawing treatments exist (A default, B high-visibility, C filled silhouette, D print-intent) — the owner's pick is outstanding.
    
*   **Custom symbols (owner direction)** — draw your own character as vector paths, assign it the Morbraii switches. Not built.
    

**Games (owner direction) — one shell, many voices, and now a falling-acorn game.** A letter is already a rhythm and a pitch, so a piano, plucked, percussive or square-wave voice makes the same message playable without changing the code. Rules the team holds: no game gets its own timing or Hz table; synthesised voices are not sampled instruments and the copy says so; playable with mouse and keyboard alone, and now by touch alone; **no game claims to improve learning, retention, instrument skill or ASL recognition — none of that is measured**. **Games, built and verified.** The Games tab carries the piano, guitar and drum voices, an ear round, a **play-along lane**, free play, and **Morbraii Mission — Acorn Tap**.

*   **The play-along lane** judges on **when** you pressed (dead-on ±90 ms, early/late ±220 ms — our own hand-tuned windows, not measurements) and on the fret (number row 1–5, or clicking a fret button); every note is the letter's own Morse element at the shared unit and pitches. Start runs a visible **1800 ms count-in** and **nothing is scored during it** — verified: a press 461 ms into the count-in leaves the tally at all zeros with the judge still reading "No key pressed yet.", and a press on the first note is judged dead-on (measured at +8 and +3 ms on two runs, fret 1, A dot, A C5 520.0 Hz). The lane's missing lead-in — the defect I measured on 2026-10-20 — is fixed in 60 lane-scoped lines; no edit touches the Morse timing table, the unit grid, the Braille cell or the letters.
    
*   **Three voices on the one shell:** **Piano** (keys skin), **Guitar** (five strings, a fretboard band, a fret strip) and **Drum** (no skin). All three are one oscillator through the same gate — no samples, nothing recorded — and each keeps its own progress bucket (`morbrai.game.piano`, `.guitar`, `.drum`). Measured: the same two-note chart in all three voices gives **identical gates, 890 ms and 1770 ms** — the element length is the code and no voice shortens it.
    
*   **Acorn Tap (owner's request, 2026-10-27; built and verified by me).** Acorns fall down a chute carrying one letter's Morse; tap the matching answer to catch one. **Mode A · read the code** shows the dots and dashes and you tap the letter; **Mode B · know the code** shows the letter and you tap the pattern. Each acorn **sounds** its letter as it falls — the letter's own elements at the shared unit and the shared pitch map, in whichever voice is picked. Verified on the served page and in the export: mode A acorn `Morse .-` → tap "Answer A" → _caught · A is .- · streak 1_; a wrong tap → _wrong tap · W is not this acorn_ with the acorn still falling; mode B acorn `letter A` → tap `.-`; separate buckets `morbrai.game.acorn.read` and `.know`. It is the first game built **for a phone**: the game played by touch at a 390 × 844 viewport with every button measured (answers 177 × 64, mode buttons 270 × 56, start 330 × 56), no hover and no keyboard needed — and I widened one text link that was 16 px tall to a proper 48 px touch target.
    
*   **The ASL seam is in place, with no camera code:** every tap is judged by one function, so a hand-based input can be wired to the same check later. **The site's promise is unchanged** — "nothing on this page reads a camera, a microphone or your hand, no ASL recognition is built here".
    

**Frets on Fire steer (owner, 2026-10-19).** After playing _Frets on Fire_, the owner asked whether practising these communication styles might **incorporatively** teach a bit of guitar and piano. The guitar game is now a voice and a skin on the same lane, and the honest framing is written into the page itself: it practises rhythm, timing and pitch discrimination; it does **not** teach guitar, drums or piano (no strings, no fret positions, no chord shapes), the voices are oscillators and not sampled instruments, and "this also starts you on an instrument" is an unmeasured hypothesis that must never appear as a claim on the site. **ASL/camera branch — scouted, and the owner has now pointed at it.** The owner says Acorn Tap should also be made for **ASL hand recognition "when we have further development on camera ASL recognition"**. The assessment stands: build the **out** direction now (Morse → fingerspelling animation from the verified assets, labelled "fingerspelling, not ASL"); keep any camera work to a **measurement-only hand-landmark bench** (21 landmarks, is-a-hand, handedness, confidence, frame rate — **no classifier, no accuracy percentages**); and hold **full sign recognition** until there is an annotated corpus, a real phone and a Deaf signer's review.
